\textbf{\large{Abstract}	}
\end{center}

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{small}

We study heterogeneous multi-teacher distillation for compact visual language models (VLMs). The setting is difficult because teachers come from different model families, use different tokenizers and provide complementary supervision across samples. Fixed teacher averaging can be ineffective, especially for sub-billion-parameter students.

We present \methodname{}, short for \methodfull{}. The framework targets three failure modes in this setting: tokenizer mismatch, heterogeneous teacher specialization and unstable post-routing weighting. To the best of our knowledge, it is the first distillation framework to employ a shared tree-metric OT for heterogeneous multi-teacher VLM distillation. It combines a trie-Wasserstein KD loss on a shared byte trie, a teacher routing network, sparse top-$k$ routing and gradient-agreement refinement. The routing network first selects a sparse teacher subset for each sample. GRACE then compares each teacher's KD gradient with the student's supervised CE gradient, smooths the agreement scores with an exponential moving average and uses these scores to form final KD weights over the routed teacher set.

In the reported TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA experiments, \methodname{} improves over direct finetuning and the compared distillation baselines. In the four-teacher setting, \methodname{} reaches 69.38, 73.11 and 80.20 on SmolVLM-500M and 60.73, 60.48 and 72.32 on SmolVLM-256M. For SmolVLM-500M, this corresponds to gains of 22.26\%, 22.83\% and 27.71\% over the original student. The method also closes 55.03\% of the original teacher gap on TextVQA, 39.53\% on DocVQA and 55.34\% on ChartQA. These results support \methodname{} as an effective distillation framework for compact text-rich VLMs under the evaluated settings.


\vspace*{1cm}
\textbf{Keywords}: visual language model, knowledge distillation, multi-teacher distillation, mixture-of-experts routing, cross-tokenizer Wasserstein distillation
\end{small}
