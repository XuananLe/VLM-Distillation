\textbf{\large{Abstract}	}
\end{center}

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{small}

We study heterogeneous multi-teacher distillation for visual language models
(VLMs), where supervision is transferred from a pool of frozen teachers to a
student model. The setting is difficult because teachers come from different
model families, use different tokenizers and provide complementary supervision
across samples, which makes fixed teacher averaging unreliable.

We present \gracename{}, short for \gracefull{}, as a framework designed to
address three failure modes in this setting: tokenizer mismatch, heterogeneous
teacher specialization and unstable post-routing weighting. In this thesis,
the frozen heterogeneous teachers are treated as tokenizer-specific experts and
the final teacher assignment is gradient-routed because routed teacher weights
are refined using agreement between each teacher's KD gradient and the
supervised CE gradient. To the best of our knowledge, it is the first
distillation framework to employ a shared tree-metric OT for heterogeneous
multi-teacher VLM distillation. It combines a trie-Wasserstein KD loss on a
shared byte trie, a teacher routing network, sparse top-$k$ routing and
gradient-agreement refinement. After the routing network selects a sparse
teacher subset for each sample, \gracename{} compares each teacher's KD
gradient with the student's supervised CE gradient, smooths the resulting
agreement scores with an exponential moving average and converts them into
final KD weights over the routed teacher set.

In the reported TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA experiments, \methodname{}
improves over direct finetuning and the compared distillation baselines. The
experimental student models are SmolVLM-500M and SmolVLM-256M. In the
four-teacher setting, \methodname{} reaches 69.38, 73.11 and 80.20 on
SmolVLM-500M and 60.73, 60.48 and 72.32 on SmolVLM-256M. For SmolVLM-500M,
this corresponds to gains of 22.26\%, 22.83\% and 27.71\% over the original
student. The method also closes 55.03\% of the original teacher gap on TextVQA,
39.53\% on DocVQA and 55.34\% on ChartQA. These results support
\methodname{} as an effective framework for heterogeneous teacher-to-student
VLM distillation in the evaluated VQA setting.


\vspace*{1cm}
\textbf{Keywords}: visual language model, knowledge distillation, multi-teacher distillation, mixture-of-experts routing, cross-tokenizer Wasserstein distillation
\end{small}
