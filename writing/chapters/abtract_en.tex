\textbf{\large{Abstract}}
\end{center}

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{small}

This thesis studies heterogeneous multi-teacher knowledge distillation for
vision-language models (VLMs). The problem is to transfer supervision from a
pool of frozen teacher models to a smaller student when the teachers come from
different model families, use different tokenizers and vary in usefulness
across samples. In this setting, direct token-index distillation is invalid and
fixed teacher averaging can mix helpful teacher signals with irrelevant or
conflicting ones.


To address this problem, the thesis proposes \gracefull{} (\gracename{}), a
distillation framework for heterogeneous teacher-to-student VLM transfer.
\gracename{} combines three mechanisms: Byte-Trie Wasserstein knowledge
distillation over a shared UTF-8 byte trie, sparse top-$k$ teacher routing from
student hidden states and gradient-agreement refinement of routed teacher
weights. The Byte-Trie Wasserstein loss makes teacher and student output
distributions comparable across tokenizers, while sparse routing and
gradient-agreement refinement select teachers relevant to each sample and weight them
by CE-gradient alignment.


Experiments on TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA evaluate \methodname{} with
SmolVLM-500M and SmolVLM-256M students under a four-teacher heterogeneous pool.
For SmolVLM-500M, \methodname{} reaches 69.38, 73.11 and 80.20 on TextVQA,
DocVQA and ChartQA, compared with original student scores of 56.75, 59.52 and
62.80. These results correspond to relative gains of 22.26\%, 22.83\% and
27.71\% over the original student and close 55.03\%, 39.53\% and 55.34\% of
the original gap to the highest-scoring teacher. For SmolVLM-256M, \methodname{}
reaches 60.73, 60.48 and 72.32 on the same benchmarks. Across the reported
experiments, \methodname{} improves over direct fine-tuning, same-family logit-based KD, cross-family ULD and the compared heterogeneous multi-teacher baselines.


\vspace*{1cm}
\textbf{Keywords:} vision-language model, knowledge distillation,
multi-teacher distillation, sparse teacher routing, gradient agreement,
cross-tokenizer Wasserstein distillation
\end{small}
