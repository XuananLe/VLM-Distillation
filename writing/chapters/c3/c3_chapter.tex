\chapter{Proposed Method}
\label{chap:proposed-method}

This chapter presents the full GRACE method. It first formulates the
multi-teacher distillation problem, then defines the trie-Wasserstein KD loss,
the student-conditioned router and the GRACE score-refinement stage. It then
gives a compact notation summary, a one-page intuition and the full training
step before the later sections expand each component. It closes with the final
training objective.

\section{Method Overview and Problem Formulation}

We consider text-rich multimodal understanding tasks that require reading image
text, understanding the question and generating an answer sequence
\cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.
The training set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}=\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{B},
\end{equation}
where \(x_b\) is a multimodal input and \(y_b\) is the target answer sequence.

Let \(S_{\theta}\) denote the student VLM with trainable parameters \(\theta\) and let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}=\{T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_N\}
\end{equation}
be a pool of frozen teacher VLMs. For each sample \(b\), the student produces logits
\(\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}\) and teacher \(T_i\) produces teacher logits
\(\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)}\).

The goal is not to pre-select a fixed teacher subset before
training. Instead, we train the student with
\emph{\methodfull{} (\methodname{})}, a routed multi-teacher distillation
framework that refines sparse teacher assignments with gradient-based scores.

\methodname{} has five components:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A supervised student objective \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\).
\item A per-teacher trie-Wasserstein objective \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\).
\item A student-conditioned MLP gate.
\item Top-\(k\) routing, capacity control and router regularization.
\item GRACE score refinement based on pooled gradient cosine similarity.
\end{enumerate}

At a high level, the gate first proposes dense teacher weights from pooled
student hidden states. A short hard-routing warmup keeps all teachers
available; after that, top-\(k\) and capacity constraints make the routing
sparse. Each routed teacher then contributes a candidate KD loss and a GRACE
score that measures directional agreement with the supervised CE update.

Before GRACE warmup, the method uses uniform KD weights over the teachers that
remain after routing. After warmup, GRACE smooths the scores with an EMA,
converts them to a score softmax, blends them with the routed weights, and
falls back to a uniform distribution only when the score spread is too small to
justify a strong preference.

The final objective combines supervised CE, routed KD and small router
regularizers. Its full form appears in Section~\ref{sec:final-objective}.

This design is closer in spirit to selective gradient-based distillation
\cite{zhu2021studentcustomized} and sparse expert routing
\cite{shazeer2017outrageouslylargeneuralnetworks}. Large-scale routed
Transformer systems such as Switch Transformers show the same need for sparse
assignment and overflow control \cite{fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion}.

Here we adapt that idea to the multi-teacher multimodal setting with token-level logit distillation losses.

The notation table, one-page intuition and early algorithm below fix the global
picture first. The detailed subsections then define the trie-Wasserstein loss,
routed teacher selection and GRACE score refinement in detail.

The method follows four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run the student to obtain \((\mathbf{z}_s,\mathbf{H}_s,\mathcal{L}_{CE})\).
\item Compute gate logits \(\mathbf r\), adjusted routing scores \(\tilde{\mathbf r}\), soft routing weights \(\mathbf p\) and route teachers under top-\(k\) and capacity constraints.
\item Compute routed per-teacher trie-Wasserstein losses \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\).
\item Compute pooled gradient-alignment scores, smooth them over time, blend them with the routed weights and apply fallback when needed.
\end{enumerate}

This four-stage view is the shortest way to read \methodname{} end to end:
route first, score routed teachers next, then form the effective KD weights
that enter the final loss.

\caption{GRACE Training Step}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Input:} mini-batch \(\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{B}\), frozen teachers \(\{T_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\)
\Statex \textbf{State:} student \(S_\theta\), teacher gate state, GRACE EMA state
\Statex \textbf{Hyperparameters:} \(k\), \(C_t\), \(\rho_h\), \(\rho_g\), \(\tau_g\), \(\beta\), \(\epsilon\), \(\alpha\), \(\lambda_{bal}\), \(\lambda_{ent}\), \(\lambda_z\), \(\eta\)
\Statex \textbf{Phase 1: Student Forward}
\State \((\mathbf{z}_s,\mathbf{H}_s,\mathcal{L}_{CE}) \gets S_\theta(x)\)
\State \(\mathbf{h} \gets \mathrm{Pool}(\mathbf{H}_s)\)
\Statex \vspace{-0.5em}
\Statex \textbf{Phase 2: Teacher Routing}
\State \((\tilde{\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{p}) \gets \mathrm{RouterScores}(\mathbf{h})\)
\State \(k_t \gets \mathrm{WarmupTopK}(t; N,k,\rho_h T)\)
\For{each sample \(b=1,\ldots,B\)}
    \State \((\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)}, \mathcal{R}_b) \gets \mathrm{Route}(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)}; k_t, C_t)\)
\EndFor
\Statex \vspace{-0.5em}
\Statex \textbf{Phase 3: KD Loss Computation}
\For{each sample \(b=1,\ldots,B\)}
    \For{each routed teacher \(i \in \mathcal{R}_b\)}
        \State \(\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)} \gets T_i(x_b)\)
        \State \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)} \gets \mathcal{L}_{TW}(\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}, \mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)}; \Omega_{b,i}^{KD})\)
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\Statex \vspace{-0.5em}
\Statex \textbf{Phase 4: GRACE Refinement and Update}
\If{\(t < \rho_g T\)}
    \State \(w^{(b)} \gets U(\mathcal{R}_b)\) for all \(b\)
\Else
    \State \((\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)},\{\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}\}) \gets \mathrm{GradientProxies}(\mathbf{z}_s,\{\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)}\})\) for all \(b\)
    \State \(\mathbf{s}^{(b)} \gets \mathrm{Align}(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)},\{\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}\})\) for all \(b\)
    \State \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{(b)} \gets \mathrm{EMA}(\mathbf{s}^{(b)})\) for all \(b\)
    \State \(\mathbf{q}^{(b)} \gets \mathrm{ScoreWeights}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{(b)}|_{\mathcal{R}_b})\) for all \(b\)
    \State \(\mathbf{w}^{(b)} \gets \mathrm{Blend}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)},\mathbf{q}^{(b)})\) for all \(b\)

\EndIf
\State \(\mathcal{L}_{KD} \gets \mathrm{BatchKD}(\{\mathbf{w}^{(b)},\mathcal{R}_b,\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\}_{b=1}^{B})\)
\State \(\mathcal{L} \gets \mathcal{L}_{CE} + \alpha\mathcal{L}_{KD} + \lambda_{bal}\mathcal{L}_{bal} + \lambda_{ent}\mathcal{L}_{ent} + \lambda_z\mathcal{L}_z\)

\end{algorithmic}

\section{Trie-Wasserstein Cross-Tokenizer Distillation}

\subsection{Supervised Answer Tokens}

We apply distillation only on supervised answer tokens, not on prompt tokens.
For sample \(b\), let \(\Omega_b^{CE}\) denote the student positions whose labels
are not ignored. For each teacher \(T_i\), let \(\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}\) denote the
matched answer positions after:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Masking ignored labels on both student and teacher sides.
\item Optionally removing EOS from the student or teacher answer span.
\item Truncating both spans to a common length.
\end{enumerate}

This produces one supervised student set for CE and one matched
student-teacher set for each KD term.

\subsection{Shared Byte Trie}

For each sample \(b\), token position \(j \in \Omega_{b,i}^{KD}\) and teacher \(T_i\), let
\(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_s}\) be the student logits and
\(\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_i}\) be the teacher logits.
The student and teacher probability distributions are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{s,j}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}}{\tau_s}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{p}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}}{\tau_t}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_s\) and \(\tau_t\) are the student and teacher temperatures.

The implementation does not compare these distributions by token ID or
sorted rank. Instead, the method decodes each single-token vocabulary entry to
a string, converts that string to UTF-8 bytes, appends an EOS sentinel, and
inserts the resulting byte path into one shared
trie. If \(e\) is a trie edge at depth \(d(e)\), its transport weight is
\begin{equation}
w(e)=
\begin{cases}
\rho^{d(e)-1}, & e \neq \mathrm{TAIL},\\
1, & e=\mathrm{TAIL},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:tw_edge_weight}
\end{equation}
where \(0<\rho<1\) is the edge-decay factor. Shared prefixes therefore receive
matching early edges, while deeper trie disagreements are discounted. The
synthetic TAIL edge is handled separately with weight \(1\)
\cite{takezawa2021supervisedtreewasserstein,peyre2019computationaloptimaltransport}.

Different tokenizers may stop at different depths, but they still share the
same byte-prefix edges when their decoded token strings are related. The trie
therefore \textbf{matches probability mass on shared byte paths instead of
token IDs.} The example in Figure~\ref{fig:grace-trie-shared} uses the pair
\texttt{document}/\texttt{receipt} and \texttt{doc}/\texttt{rece}.

\subsection{Sparse \texorpdfstring{Top-\(k\)}{Top-k} Support and Tail Edge}

For efficiency, the loss keeps only the top-\(k_{TW}\) non-ignored student and
teacher tokens at each supervised position. Let
\(\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{(b)}\) and \(\mathcal{K}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\) be those retained
token sets. The code then collapses the remaining probability mass into a
dedicated TAIL edge:
\begin{equation}
r_{s,j}^{(b)}
=
1-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{(b)}} p_{s,j}^{(b)}(v),
\qquad
r_{t,j}^{(b,i)}
=
1-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}} p_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(v).
\end{equation}

For any active trie edge \(e\), the retained subtree masses are
\begin{equation}
m_{s,j}^{(b)}(e)
=
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{(b)}:\,e\in\mathrm{path}(v)} p_{s,j}^{(b)}(v)
\;+\;
\mathbf{1}[e=\mathrm{TAIL}]\,r_{s,j}^{(b)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
m_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(e)
=
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}:\,e\in\mathrm{path}(v)} p_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(v)
\;+\;
\mathbf{1}[e=\mathrm{TAIL}]\,r_{t,j}^{(b,i)}.
\end{equation}
The token-level trie-Wasserstein loss is then
\begin{equation}
\ell_{TW}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)},\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right)
=
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{act,j}^{(b,i)}}
w(e)\,
\left|
m_{s,j}^{(b)}(e)-m_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(e)
\right|,
\label{eq:token_tw}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_{act,j}^{(b,i)}\) contains the active student edges, active
teacher edges and the TAIL edge.

The per-sample, per-teacher KD loss remains the mean over supervised answer
tokens:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}
=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}|}
\sum_{j\in\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}}
\ell_{TW}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)},\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right).
\label{eq:per_teacher_tw}
\end{equation}

Compared with the ULD baseline from Chapter~\ref{chap:preliminaries}, this
loss compares mass on shared trie edges instead of ranked logits. It keeps the
cross-tokenizer goal, but it uses a structured transport view that is closer to
the actual tokenizer geometry, while the top-\(k\)+TAIL approximation keeps the
cost bounded by the active support.

\section{Learned Teacher Gate and Load Balancing}

\subsection{Student-Conditioned Router}

When multiple teachers are available, the method first builds a student-conditioned router instead of averaging them uniformly.

Let \(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\) denote the student hidden states captured at the routing source layer for sample \(b\). We pool these hidden states over supervised answer positions to obtain a routing feature
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}^{(b)} = \mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}
In code, this pool is the mean over supervised answer positions. If a sample
has no supervised answer token, the gate falls back to the positions selected by
the attention mask.

A student-conditioned MLP router \(G(\cdot)\) then produces gate logits
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}^{(b)} = G\!\left(\mathbf{h}^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}
The code then forms routing scores
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)}
=
\frac{\mathbf{r}^{(b)} + \mathbf{b} + \boldsymbol{\xi}^{(b)}}{\tau_g},
\qquad
\mathbf{p}^{(b)} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)}\right),
\label{eq:router-softmax}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{b}\) is the expert-bias vector, \(\tau_g\) is the router
temperature and \(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(b)}\) is Gaussian router noise during
training. Here \(p_i^{(b)}\) is the initial gate weight for teacher \(T_i\).

The gate stack in Figure~\ref{fig:grace-gate-balance} applies LayerNorm
\(\rightarrow\) up projection \(\rightarrow\) split into gate and value branches
\(\rightarrow\) SiLU(gate) \(\odot\) value \(\rightarrow\) down projection, then
applies bias, top-$k$, capacity control, fallback and balance updates to the
resulting teacher logits.



\subsection{Top-k, Capacity and Fallback}

Let \(k_t\) denote the effective top-\(k\) value at training step \(t\). The
router is made sparse by applying top-\(k\) routing to the routing
scores \(\tilde{r}_i^{(b)}\). Let
\(\mathcal{K}_b=\mathrm{TopK}(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)},k_t)\) be the candidate
teacher set for sample \(b\). To prevent one teacher from receiving too many
assignments, each teacher is additionally limited by a capacity
\begin{equation}
C_t = \left\lceil \phi \frac{Bk_t}{N}\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
where \(B\) is the mini-batch size, \(N\) is the number of teachers, and
\(\phi > 0\) is the capacity factor.

Let \(m_i^{(b)} \in \{0,1\}\) denote the resulting assignment mask after top-\(k\)
selection, capacity pruning and fallback. The constrained gate weights are
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{m_i^{(b)} p_i^{(b)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} m_j^{(b)} p_j^{(b)}}.
\label{eq:constrained_gate_weights}
\end{equation}

If capacity pruning leaves a sample without any assigned teacher, the algorithm falls back to the teacher with the largest bias-adjusted routing score \(\tilde{r}_i^{(b)}\).

\textbf{This ensures that at least one teacher remains available before GRACE score refinement.}

The implementation also uses a short hard-routing warmup. Let
\(\rho_h\) be the hard-routing warmup ratio. During the first \(\rho_h T\)
steps, the router uses
\begin{equation}
k_t = N,
\end{equation}
so all teachers remain available. After that point, the router switches to the
configured sparse value \(k_t = k\).

\subsection{Balancing Loss and Expert Bias Update}

The full GRACE framework regularizes the gate with a balancing
loss that uses both soft routing weights and hard assignments.

Define the soft load
\begin{equation}
\bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} p_i^{(b)}
\end{equation}
and the hard assignment load
\begin{equation}
\bar{q}_i
=
\frac{\sum_{b=1}^{B} m_i^{(b)}}{\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{j=1}^{N} m_j^{(b)}},
\end{equation}
The balancing objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{bal}
=
N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{p}_i\bar{q}_i.
\label{eq:balance_loss}
\end{equation}

The expert bias is updated from the per-batch assignment load
\(\ell_i\) to discourage teacher overuse:
\begin{equation}
b_i \leftarrow b_i - \eta_b \left(\ell_i - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\ell_j\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_b\) is a small bias-update rate.

The code also adds two small auxiliary router penalties:
\begin{itemize}
\item A negative-entropy term
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{ent}
=
-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} H(\mathbf{p}^{(b)}),
\end{equation}
which discourages premature routing collapse.
\item A router \(z\)-loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{z}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\left(\log \sum_{i=1}^{N} e^{r_i^{(b)}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
which keeps router logits numerically well behaved.
\end{itemize}

\section{GRACE Score Refinement}

\subsection{Gradient Proxies}

For each sample \(b\), let \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}^{(b)}\) be the student cross-entropy loss on supervised answer tokens.
We define the pooled CE logit-gradient proxy as
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)}
=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_b^{CE}|}
\sum_{j\in\Omega_b^{CE}}
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}} \mathcal{L}_{CE}^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

In implementation, the code computes this gradient in closed form from the student
probabilities and labels, which avoids a second backward pass through the full
model.

For each teacher \(T_i\), the pooled KD logit-gradient proxy is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}
=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_b^{CE}|}
\sum_{j\in\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}}
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\end{equation}

The code computes this proxy directly from the selected distillation loss. In the
default setting here, \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\) is the
trie-Wasserstein loss in Equation~\ref{eq:per_teacher_tw}. The KD sum uses the
matched student-teacher positions \(\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}\), but the normalization
still uses the student CE count \(|\Omega_b^{CE}|\).

\subsection{GRACE Scores and Logged Active Flags}

For each sample \(b\) and teacher \(T_i\), we measure directional agreement between supervised learning and KD by cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
s_i^{(b)}
=
\cos\!\left(
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)},
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}
\right).
\end{equation}

The CE proxy averages over \(\Omega_b^{CE}\), while the KD proxy sums over
\(\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}\) and normalizes by \(|\Omega_b^{CE}|\). This matches the
current implementation.

The implementation also records a thresholded activity flag
\begin{equation}
a_i^{(b)} = \mathbf{1}[s_i^{(b)} > \delta],
\end{equation}
where \(\delta\) is a logging threshold. These flags are useful for monitoring,
but the GRACE weight update itself uses the continuous scores \(s_i^{(b)}\).
Figure~\ref{fig:grace-directional-view} gives the directional picture behind
this cosine score.

\subsection{Warmup, Score Smoothing and Gradient Weights}

GRACE refinement starts only after a warmup phase. Let \(\rho_g \in [0,1]\)
denote the GRACE warmup ratio with respect to the total number of training
steps. During the first \(\rho_g T\) steps, the code uses a uniform KD
distribution over the teachers that remain after routing:
\begin{equation}
u_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\mathbf{1}[\,\hat{p}_i^{(b)} > 0\,]}
{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}[\,\hat{p}_j^{(b)} > 0\,]}.
\label{eq:uniform_routed_weights}
\end{equation}

After warmup, GRACE refines the routed teachers with a score-smoothed gradient
distribution. Let \(\bar{s}_i^{\mathrm{prev}}\) be the running exponential
moving average (EMA) of past batch means for teacher \(T_i\) and let \(\gamma\)
be the EMA decay. The per-sample smoothed score is
\begin{equation}
\hat{s}_i^{(b)}
=
\gamma \bar{s}_i^{\mathrm{prev}}
+
(1-\gamma) s_i^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/grace_ema_smoothing.png}
    \caption[EMA smoothing in GRACE.]{EMA smoothing in \methodname{}.}
    \label{fig:grace-ema-smoothing}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Raw per-batch scores are noisy, while smoothed scores produce more
stable teacher weights,} a point illustrated by
Figure~\ref{fig:grace-ema-smoothing}.

Let \(\mathcal{R}_b=\{i \mid \hat{p}_i^{(b)} > 0\}\) be the routed teacher set.
GRACE converts the smoothed scores into a gradient-weight vector
\begin{equation}
q_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{
\exp(\beta \hat{s}_i^{(b)}) \mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{R}_b]
}{
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\exp(\beta \hat{s}_j^{(b)}) \mathbf{1}[j \in \mathcal{R}_b]
},
\label{eq:grace_softmax}
\end{equation}
where \(\beta\) controls how sharply the highest-scoring teacher is preferred.

\end{figure}

\subsection{Router-Gradient Blend and Uniform Fallback}

After warmup, GRACE combines the routed weights and the gradient weights by a
geometric blend. Let \(\lambda \in [0,1]\) be the router-blend exponent. The
unnormalized blended weights are
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_i^{(b)}
\propto
\left(\hat{p}_i^{(b)}\right)^{\lambda}
\left(q_i^{(b)}\right)^{1-\lambda},
\end{equation}
followed by normalization over the routed teachers:
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\tilde{w}_i^{(b)} \mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{R}_b]}
{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_j^{(b)} \mathbf{1}[j \in \mathcal{R}_b]}.
\label{eq:effective_teacher_weights}
\end{equation}

The implementation uses a uniform fallback only when the smoothed scores are too
close to justify a strong preference. Define the score spread
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{(b)}
=
\max_{i \in \mathcal{R}_b}\hat{s}_i^{(b)}
-
\min_{i \in \mathcal{R}_b}\hat{s}_i^{(b)}.
\end{equation}
If \(\Delta^{(b)} < \epsilon\), then GRACE falls back to
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{R}_b]}
{|\mathcal{R}_b|}.
\label{eq:alignment_fallback}
\end{equation}
\textbf{This keeps KD stable when the routed teachers have nearly indistinguishable
GRACE scores.}

\subsection{Sample-Wise KD Aggregation}

The routed KD loss for sample \(b\) is the weighted aggregation
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i^{(b)} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\label{eq:sample_kd_average}
\end{equation}

We then average these sample losses over the batch:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}.
\label{eq:batch_kd}
\end{equation}

This rule has two stages: the gate defines the routed teacher set and GRACE
refines those routed teachers into the final KD weights.

\section{Final Objective}
\label{sec:final-objective}

The final optimization objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{CE}
+
\alpha\mathcal{L}_{KD}
+
\lambda_{bal}\mathcal{L}_{bal}
+
\lambda_{ent}\mathcal{L}_{ent}
+
\lambda_{z}\mathcal{L}_{z},
\label{eq:final_objective}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\) is the standard supervised next-token cross-entropy loss and
\(\mathcal{L}_{KD}\) is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:batch_kd}. The balance term
\(\mathcal{L}_{bal}\) is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:balance_loss}; the entropy
term \(\mathcal{L}_{ent}\) and router \(z\)-loss \(\mathcal{L}_{z}\) are small
penalties on the teacher gate.

Equation~\ref{eq:final_objective} keeps the student anchored to ground-truth
supervision while adding routed KD and small router regularizers. Compared with
static multi-teacher averaging, this objective makes the teacher contribution
adaptive at the sample level. Compared with fixed teacher-subset selection, it
avoids committing to one subset for the whole run
\cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,zhu2021studentcustomized}.

Together with the early algorithmic summary, Equation~\ref{eq:final_objective}
completes the formal training recipe used in the experiments.
