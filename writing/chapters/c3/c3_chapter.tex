\chapter{Proposed Method}
\label{chap:proposed-method}

This chapter presents the full GRACE method. It first formulates the
multi-teacher distillation problem, then defines the trie-Wasserstein KD loss,
the student-conditioned router and the GRACE score-refinement stage. It then
gives a compact notation summary and the full training step before the later
sections expand each component. It closes with the final training objective.

\section{Method Overview and Problem Formulation}

We consider text-rich multimodal understanding tasks that require reading image
text, understanding the question and generating an answer sequence
\cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.
The training set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}=\{(x_n,y_n)\}_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{data}}},
\end{equation}
where \(x_n\) is a multimodal input and \(y_n\) is the target answer sequence.

Let \(S_{\theta}\) denote the student VLM with trainable parameters \(\theta\),
and let \(\mathcal{T}=\{T_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\) be the frozen teacher pool. For each
sample \(b\), the student produces logits \(\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}\) and teacher
\(T_i\) produces teacher logits \(\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)}\).

The goal is not to pre-select a fixed teacher subset before
training. Instead, we train the student with
\emph{\methodfull{} (\methodname{})}, a routed multi-teacher distillation
framework that refines sparse teacher assignments with gradient-based scores.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/GRACE-Architecture.pdf}
\caption[GRACE architecture.]{GRACE architecture. The student produces logits and hidden states, the router selects candidate teachers, trie-Wasserstein KD computes per-teacher losses and GRACE refinement produces final KD weights.}
\label{fig:grace-architecture}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:grace-architecture} summarizes the full training path. The
method is defined through four operators:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Trie-Wasserstein KD:} computes per-teacher cross-tokenizer
distillation losses on a shared byte trie.
\item \textbf{Student-conditioned routing:} selects candidate teachers from
student-side hidden states.
\item \textbf{GRACE score refinement:} adjusts routed teacher weights using
parameter-gradient agreement.
\item \textbf{Final objective:} combines supervised CE, routed KD and router
auxiliary terms.
\end{itemize}

GRACE adapts sparse expert routing
\cite{shazeer2017outrageouslylargeneuralnetworks,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion}
to heterogeneous multi-teacher VLM distillation with token-level logit
supervision. It is also related to selective gradient-based distillation
\cite{zhu2021studentcustomized}.

Table~\ref{tab:notation} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:training-step} summarize the
notation and training step. The detailed subsections then define the
trie-Wasserstein loss, routed teacher selection and GRACE score refinement in
detail.

The method follows four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run the student to obtain \((\mathbf{z}_s,\mathbf{H}_s,\mathcal{L}_{CE})\).
\item Compute gate logits \(\mathbf r\), soft routing weights \(\mathbf p\) and route teachers with top-\(k\) masking.
\item Compute per-teacher trie-Wasserstein losses \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\).
\item Compute parameter-gradient agreement scores, smooth them over time, blend them with the routed weights and apply fallback when needed.
\end{enumerate}

This four-stage view is the shortest way to read \methodname{} end to end:
route first, score teacher candidates next, then form the effective KD weights
that enter the final loss.

\subsection{Training Step}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{GRACE Training Step}
\label{alg:training-step}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Input:} mini-batch \(\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{B}\), teachers \(\mathcal{T}\)
\Statex \textbf{State:} student \(S_\theta\), teacher gate, GRACE EMA
\Statex \textbf{Key hyperparameters:} \(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\), \(k_{\mathrm{route}}\), \(\rho_g\), \(\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}}\), \(\beta_{\mathrm{score}}\), \(\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}}\)
\Statex \textbf{Phase 1: student forward}
\State \((\mathbf{z}_s,\mathbf{H}_s,\mathcal{L}_{CE}) \gets S_\theta(x)\)
\State \(\mathbf{h} \gets \mathrm{Pool}(\mathbf{H}_s)\)
\Statex \textbf{Phase 2: teacher routing}
\State \((\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p}) \gets \mathrm{RouterScores}(\mathbf{h})\)
\For{each sample \(b=1,\ldots,B\)}
    \State \((\mathcal{R}_b,\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)}) \gets \mathrm{TopKRoute}(\mathbf{r}^{(b)},\mathbf{p}^{(b)};k_{\mathrm{route}})\)
\EndFor
\Statex \textbf{Phase 3: KD loss computation}
\For{each teacher \(i=1,\ldots,N\)}
    \State \(\mathbf{z}_t^{(:,i)} \gets T_i(x)\)
    \For{each sample \(b=1,\ldots,B\)}
        \State \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)} \gets \mathcal{L}_{TW}(\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)},\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)})\)
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\Statex \textbf{Phase 4: GRACE refinement and update}
\If{\(t < \rho_g T_{\mathrm{train}}\)}
    \State \(\mathbf{w}^{(b)} \gets \hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)}\) for all \(b\)
\Else
    \State \(\mathbf{g}_{CE} \gets \nabla_{\Theta}\mathcal{L}_{CE}\)
    \For{each teacher \(i=1,\ldots,N\)}
        \State \(\mathbf{g}_i \gets \nabla_{\Theta}\mathrm{Mean}_b(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)})\)
        \State \(s_i \gets \cos(\mathbf{g}_{CE},\mathbf{g}_i)\), \quad \(\tilde{a}_i \gets \mathbf{1}[s_i>\delta]\)
    \EndFor
    \State \(\hat{\mathbf{s}} \gets \mathrm{EMA}_{\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}}}(\mathbf{s})\)
    \For{each sample \(b=1,\ldots,B\)}
        \State \(\mathcal{A}_b \gets \{i\in\mathcal{R}_b:\tilde{a}_i=1\}\)
        \State \(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{(b)} \gets \mathrm{ScoreSoftmax}(\hat{\mathbf{s}},\mathcal{A}_b;\beta_{\mathrm{score}})\)
        \State \(\mathbf{w}^{(b)} \gets \mathrm{BlendOrFallback}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)},\boldsymbol{\omega}^{(b)})\)
    \EndFor
\EndIf
\State \(\mathcal{L}_{KD} \gets \mathrm{BatchKD}(\mathbf{w},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}_{KD})\)
\State \(\mathcal{L} \gets \mathcal{L}_{CE} + \alpha_{\mathrm{KD}}\mathcal{L}_{KD} + \lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}\mathcal{L}_{ent} + \lambda_z\mathcal{L}_z\)
\State \(\theta \gets \mathrm{Update}(\theta,\mathcal{L},\eta)\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Here \(\mathrm{RouterScores}\) refers to Equation~\ref{eq:router-softmax}, and
\(\mathrm{BatchKD}\) refers to Equation~\ref{eq:batch_kd}. The remaining
shorthands denote the GRACE scoring, active-set weighting, warmup and fallback
rules defined below.

\section{Trie-Wasserstein Cross-Tokenizer Distillation}

\subsection{Supervised Answer Tokens}

We apply distillation only on supervised answer tokens, not on prompt tokens.
For sample \(b\), let \(\Omega_b^{CE}\) denote the student positions whose labels
are not ignored. For each teacher \(T_i\), let \(\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}\) denote the
matched answer positions after:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Masking ignored labels on both student and teacher sides.
\item Optionally removing EOS from the student or teacher answer span.
\item Truncating both spans to a common length.
\end{enumerate}

This produces one supervised student set for CE and one matched
student-teacher set for each KD term.
This is a position-wise alignment approximation across tokenizers: after
answer-span preprocessing, the \(j\)-th student and teacher answer positions are
treated as corresponding positions. The trie-Wasserstein term handles
token-distribution mismatch at each matched position, but it does not perform a
separate byte-level sequence alignment.

\subsection{Shared Byte Trie}

For each sample \(b\), token position \(j \in \Omega_{b,i}^{KD}\) and teacher \(T_i\), let
\(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_s}\) be the student logits and
\(\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_i}\) be the teacher logits.
The student and teacher probability distributions are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{s,j}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}}{\tau_s}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{p}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}}{\tau_t}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_s\) and \(\tau_t\) are the student and teacher temperatures.

Teacher and student vocabularies may differ, so direct token-index matching is
not meaningful. GRACE instead maps decoded teacher and student token strings
into a shared UTF-8 byte trie. Tokens with common byte prefixes share the same
initial trie path, even when their tokenizer IDs differ.

Each token string is converted to a byte sequence and terminated with an EOS
sentinel. The EOS marker separates complete tokens from strict prefixes, so
tokens such as \texttt{doc} and \texttt{document} remain distinct leaves even
though they share an initial path.

Let \(e\) denote a normal byte-trie or EOS edge and let \(d(e)\) be its depth.
GRACE assigns edge weights
\begin{equation}
w(e)=\rho_{\mathrm{trie}}^{d(e)-1},
\label{eq:tw_edge_weight}
\end{equation}
where \(0<\rho_{\mathrm{trie}}<1\). The first edge from the root has weight
\(1\), and deeper edges decay geometrically. This makes shared prefixes cheaper
than unrelated byte paths while preserving a valid tree metric
\cite{takezawa2021supervisedtreewasserstein,peyre2019computationaloptimaltransport}.

Different tokenizers may stop at different depths, but they still share the
same byte-prefix edges when their decoded token strings are related. The trie
therefore \textbf{matches probability mass on shared byte paths instead of
token IDs.} The example in Figure~\ref{fig:grace-trie-shared} uses the pair
\texttt{document}/\texttt{receipt} and \texttt{doc}/\texttt{rece}.

\subsection{Full Trie-Wasserstein Objective}

The shared byte trie induces a tree metric over decoded token strings.
Therefore, the \(W_1\) optimal transport distance between teacher and student
token distributions on this trie can be written as a weighted sum of
subtree-mass imbalances:
\begin{equation}
W_T(p_s,p_t)
=
\sum_{e\in E}
w(e)\left|m_s(e)-m_t(e)\right|.
\label{eq:closed_form_tw}
\end{equation}
A formal proof is provided in
Appendix~\ref{app:tree-wasserstein-proof}. The sparse construction below
applies this formula to an active support for efficiency.

\subsection{Sparse Support and Residual Tail Edge}

Equation~\ref{eq:closed_form_tw} is exact on the complete shared trie, but
evaluating all vocabulary tokens is expensive. GRACE therefore applies the same
subtree-mass formula on a sparse augmented trie.

For each aligned answer position, the sparse construction proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Retain the top-\(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\) student and teacher tokens after masking
ignored vocabulary entries.
\item Insert the retained tokens into the shared byte trie and keep their exact
trie paths.
\item Collapse the remaining non-top-\(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\) probability mass into a
single residual tail edge.
\item Compute the trie-Wasserstein loss on the resulting sparse augmented trie.
\end{enumerate}

This construction is exact for the collapsed sparse augmented trie, but it is an
approximation to the full-trie distance because all non-top-\(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\)
mass is represented by one tail atom.

Let \(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\) denote the sparse trie support size. Let
\(\mathcal{V}_s^\star\) and \(\mathcal{V}_t^\star\) denote the non-ignored
student and teacher vocabularies. The probabilities below are renormalized over
these non-ignored vocabularies. Let
\(\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{(b)}\subset\mathcal{V}_s^\star\) and
\(\mathcal{K}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\subset\mathcal{V}_t^\star\) be the retained token
sets. The residual mass assigned to the tail edge is
\begin{equation}
r_{s,j}^{(b)}
=
1-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{(b)}}p_{s,j}^{(b)}(v),
\qquad
r_{t,j}^{(b,i)}
=
1-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{K}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}}p_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(v).
\end{equation}

Let \(\pi(v)\) be the full trie path for token \(v\), and let
\(e_{\mathrm{tail}}\) be the global residual tail edge with
\(w(e_{\mathrm{tail}})=1\). For any active trie edge \(e\), the sparse subtree
masses are
\begin{equation}
m_{s,j}^{(b)}(e)
=
\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{(b)}\\e\in\pi(v)}}
p_{s,j}^{(b)}(v)
\;+\;
\mathbf{1}[e=e_{\mathrm{tail}}]\,r_{s,j}^{(b)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
m_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(e)
=
\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal{K}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\\e\in\pi(v)}}
p_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(v)
\;+\;
\mathbf{1}[e=e_{\mathrm{tail}}]\,r_{t,j}^{(b,i)}.
\end{equation}
The token-level trie-Wasserstein loss is then
\begin{equation}
\ell_{TW}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)},\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right)
=
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{act,j}^{(b,i)}}
w(e)\,
\left|
m_{s,j}^{(b)}(e)-m_{t,j}^{(b,i)}(e)
\right|,
\label{eq:token_tw}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{E}_{act,j}^{(b,i)}\) contains the active student edges, active
teacher edges and the residual tail edge.

The per-sample, per-teacher KD loss remains the mean over supervised answer
tokens:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}
=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}|}
\sum_{j\in\Omega_{b,i}^{KD}}
\ell_{TW}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)},\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right).
\label{eq:per_teacher_tw}
\end{equation}

The tail edge preserves probability mass outside the top-\(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\)
support without materializing every vocabulary path. It also keeps the runtime
state and logged diagnostics compact: the loss tracks retained exact mass and
residual tail mass.

\paragraph{Complexity Analysis.}
The shared byte trie is built once with time and memory cost
\(O(V_s \bar{L}_s + V_t \bar{L}_t)\), where \(V_s\) and \(V_t\) are the
student and teacher vocabulary sizes and \(\bar{L}_s\) and \(\bar{L}_t\) are
the average byte-path lengths. For one aligned token position, the sparse
trie-Wasserstein loss costs
\(O(V_s \log K_{\mathrm{TW}} + V_t \log K_{\mathrm{TW}} + A \log A)\), where
\(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\) is the trie support size and \(A\) is the number of active
trie edges.


Runtime memory is \(O(E + P_s + P_t + A)\), where
\(E\) is the number of trie edges, \(P_s\) and \(P_t\) are the stored student
and teacher path-index arrays. Unlike dense OT, the sparse computation does not
materialize a \(|V_s| \times |V_t|\) transport matrix.

Compared with the ULD baseline from Chapter~\ref{chap:preliminaries}, this
loss compares mass on shared trie edges instead of ranked logits. It keeps the
cross-tokenizer goal, but it uses a structured transport view that is closer to
the actual tokenizer geometry, while the top-\(K_{\mathrm{TW}}\)+tail
approximation keeps the cost bounded by the active support.

\section{Student-Conditioned Teacher Routing}

\subsection{Student-Conditioned Router}

When multiple teachers are available, the method first builds a
student-conditioned router instead of averaging them uniformly. This routing
branch is defined for \(N\ge2\); single-teacher distillation bypasses the gate
and applies the KD loss directly to the only teacher. This thesis uses the gate
for SmolVLM-style students whose language-model head is an \texttt{nn.Linear}
layer with input width equal to the text hidden size.

Let \(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\) denote the student hidden states captured immediately
before the language-model head for sample \(b\). We pool these hidden states
over supervised answer positions to obtain a routing feature
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}^{(b)} = \mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}
This pool is the mean over supervised answer positions. The routing feature is
defined for samples with at least one supervised answer token, as ensured by
the training data construction.

A student-conditioned MLP router \(G(\cdot)\) then produces gate logits
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}^{(b)} = G\!\left(\mathbf{h}^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}
The routing step then forms scores
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}^{(b)} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\mathbf{r}^{(b)}\right),
\label{eq:router-softmax}
\end{equation}
where \(p_i^{(b)}\) is the initial gate weight for teacher \(T_i\).
The gate stack uses LayerNorm, an up projection, a gate-value split, SiLU
gating and a down projection. The routing step then applies softmax to the raw
teacher logits, masks to the top-\(k_{\mathrm{route}}\) teachers and renormalizes
the routed weights.



\subsection{Top-K Routing}

After computing soft routing weights, GRACE converts them into a sparse routed
set. For sample \(b\), the routing step uses two deterministic stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Top-\(k\) candidate selection:}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_b=\mathrm{TopK}(\mathbf{r}^{(b)},\min(k_{\mathrm{route}},N)).
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Routed-weight renormalization:} the routed set is
\(\mathcal{R}_b=\mathcal{C}_b\), and all non-selected teacher weights are set
to zero before renormalization.
\end{enumerate}

Let \(m_i^{(b)}=\mathbf{1}[i\in\mathcal{R}_b]\) denote the top-\(k\)
assignment mask. The routed gate weights are
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{m_i^{(b)} p_i^{(b)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} m_j^{(b)} p_j^{(b)}}.
\label{eq:constrained_gate_weights}
\end{equation}

The result is a routed teacher set \(\mathcal{R}_b\) and normalized routed
weights \(\hat{\mathbf p}^{(b)}\). This ensures that at least one teacher
remains available before GRACE score refinement.

\subsection{Router Entropy and Z-Loss}

GRACE uses two auxiliary router terms:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Entropy term:} discourages collapse to an overly sharp routing
distribution.
\item \textbf{\(z\)-loss:} penalizes large router logits and stabilizes the
gate.
\end{itemize}
The router terms are
\begin{itemize}
\item A negative-entropy term
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{ent}
=
-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} H(\mathbf{p}^{(b)}),
\end{equation}
which discourages premature routing collapse.
\item A router \(z\)-loss, which keeps router logits numerically well behaved,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{z}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\left(\log \sum_{i=1}^{N} e^{r_i^{(b)}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

These terms regularize the teacher-selection mechanism; they do not replace the
supervised CE or KD losses.
\section{GRACE Score Refinement}

As discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:gradient-conflict-teacher-agreement},
routed teachers may still conflict with the supervised objective after teacher
selection. \methodname{} addresses this issue by refining routed teacher weights
using gradient-based agreement scores.

\subsection{Parameter-Gradient Agreement}

Let \(\Theta\subseteq\theta\) denote the trainable student parameters used by the
optimizer. GRACE scores each teacher by comparing the student parameter
gradient induced by supervised CE with the parameter gradient induced by that
teacher's KD loss. The CE gradient is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}_{CE}
=
\nabla_{\Theta}\mathcal{L}_{CE}.
\end{equation}
For teacher \(T_i\), the implementation computes the KD gradient from that
teacher's mean KD loss over the full mini-batch:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}_{i}
=
\nabla_{\Theta}
\left(
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}
\right).
\end{equation}
In this thesis, \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\) is the trie-Wasserstein loss from
Equation~\ref{eq:per_teacher_tw}. These gradients are used only to score teacher
compatibility; the final parameter update still uses the full loss in
Section~\ref{sec:final-objective}.
\subsection{Agreement Scores and Active Set}

For each teacher \(T_i\), GRACE measures directional agreement between
supervised learning and KD by cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
s_i
=
\frac{
\langle \mathbf{g}_{CE},\mathbf{g}_i\rangle
}{
\|\mathbf{g}_{CE}\|_2\|\mathbf{g}_i\|_2+\epsilon_{\mathrm{num}}
}.
\end{equation}

The agreement score \(s_i\) is teacher-level within the current mini-batch; the
same teacher score is broadcast across samples. Sample dependence enters later
through the routed set \(\mathcal{R}_b\), routed router weights
\(\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)}\) and active set \(\mathcal{A}_b\).

The active set is defined in two steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Convert the teacher-level agreement score into a binary teacher flag:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{a}_i = \mathbf{1}[s_i > \delta],
\end{equation}
where \(\delta\) is the GRACE threshold.

\item Intersect this flag with the sample-specific routed set:
\begin{equation}
a_i^{(b)}=\mathbf{1}[i\in\mathcal{R}_b]\tilde{a}_i,
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_b=\{i\in\mathcal{R}_b:a_i^{(b)}=1\}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Thus, \(s_i\) is teacher-level within the mini-batch, while \(\mathcal{A}_b\)
remains sample-specific through \(\mathcal{R}_b\). GRACE intentionally
thresholds the current raw agreement score; the score-softmax and fallback
spread below use the EMA-smoothed score.

\subsection{Warmup, EMA Smoothing and Score Weights}

GRACE refinement starts only after a warmup phase. Let \(\rho_g \in [0,1]\)
denote the GRACE warmup ratio with respect to the total number of training
steps. During the first \(\rho_g T_{\mathrm{train}}\) steps, GRACE leaves the
routed gate weights unchanged:
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}=\hat{p}_i^{(b)}.
\label{eq:grace_warmup_router_weights}
\end{equation}

After warmup, GRACE refines the routed teachers with a smoothed score
distribution. Let \(\bar{s}_i^{\mathrm{prev}}\) be the running exponential
moving average (EMA) of past batch means for teacher \(T_i\) and let
\(\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}}\) be the EMA decay. The smoothed score is
\begin{equation}
\hat{s}_i
=
\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}} \bar{s}_i^{\mathrm{prev}}
+
(1-\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}}) s_i.
\end{equation}

Raw per-batch scores are noisy, while smoothed scores produce more stable
teacher weights. If \(\mathcal{A}_b=\emptyset\), GRACE keeps the routed weights
\(\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)}\). Otherwise, it converts smoothed scores into a
score-softmax weight vector over \(\mathcal{A}_b\):
\begin{equation}
\omega_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{
\exp(\beta_{\mathrm{score}}\hat{s}_i) \mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{A}_b]
}{
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\exp(\beta_{\mathrm{score}}\hat{s}_j) \mathbf{1}[j \in \mathcal{A}_b]
},
\label{eq:grace_softmax}
\end{equation}
Here \(\beta_{\mathrm{score}}\) controls how sharply the highest-scoring active
teacher is preferred.

\subsection{Router-Score Blend and Fallback}

GRACE converts routed teachers into final KD weights through three cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No active teacher:} if \(\mathcal{A}_b=\emptyset\), retain the
routed weights:
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}=\hat{p}_i^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Small score spread:} if active teachers exist but their smoothed
scores are too close, use uniform weights over the active set. The score spread
is
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{(b)}
=
\max_{i \in \mathcal{A}_b}\hat{s}_i
-
\min_{i \in \mathcal{A}_b}\hat{s}_i.
\end{equation}
If \(\mathcal{A}_b\neq\emptyset\) and
\(\Delta^{(b)} < \epsilon_{\mathrm{fb}}\), then
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{A}_b]}
{|\mathcal{A}_b|}.
\label{eq:alignment_fallback}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Confident score spread:} otherwise, blend router weights with
GRACE score weights. Let \(\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}} \in [0,1]\) be the
router-blend exponent. The unnormalized blended weights are
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_i^{(b)}
\propto
\left(\hat{p}_i^{(b)}\right)^{\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}}}
\left(\omega_i^{(b)}\right)^{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}}},
\end{equation}
followed by normalization over the active routed teachers:
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\tilde{w}_i^{(b)} \mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{A}_b]}
{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_j^{(b)} \mathbf{1}[j \in \mathcal{A}_b]}.
\label{eq:effective_teacher_weights}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sample-Wise KD Aggregation}

The final KD loss for sample \(b\) is the weighted sum of routed per-teacher
losses:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}
=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{R}_b} w_i^{(b)} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\label{eq:sample_kd_average}
\end{equation}

The mini-batch KD term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}.
\label{eq:batch_kd}
\end{equation}

\section{Final Objective}
\label{sec:final-objective}

The final optimization objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{CE}
+
\alpha_{\mathrm{KD}}\mathcal{L}_{KD}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}\mathcal{L}_{ent}
+
\lambda_{z}\mathcal{L}_{z},
\label{eq:final_objective}
\end{equation}
The terms have distinct roles:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\) anchors the student to supervised answers.
\item \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}\), defined in Equation~\ref{eq:batch_kd}, transfers
routed teacher supervision.
\item \(\mathcal{L}_{ent}\) and \(\mathcal{L}_{z}\) regularize the teacher
gate.
\end{itemize}

Together with Algorithm~\ref{alg:training-step}, Equation~\ref{eq:final_objective}
specifies the training objective used in the experiments.
