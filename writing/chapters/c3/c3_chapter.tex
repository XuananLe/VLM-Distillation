\section{Method Overview and Problem Formulation}

We consider text-rich multimodal understanding tasks such as TextVQA, DocVQA, and ChartQA
\cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.
The training set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}=\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{B},
\end{equation}
where \(x_b\) is a multimodal input and \(y_b\) is the target answer sequence.

Let \(S_{\theta}\) denote the student VLM with trainable parameters \(\theta\), and let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}=\{T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_N\}
\end{equation}
be a pool of frozen teacher VLMs. For each sample \(b\), the student produces logits
\(\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}\), and teacher \(T_i\) produces teacher logits
\(\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)}\).

The goal of this thesis is not to pre-select a fixed teacher subset before
training. Instead, the student is trained with
\emph{\methodfull{} (\methodname{})}, a routed multi-teacher distillation
framework that refines sparse teacher assignments with gradient-based scores.

\methodname{} has five components:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a supervised student objective \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\),
\item a per-teacher ULD objective \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\),
\item a deep student-conditioned gate,
\item top-\(k\) routing, capacity control, and optional router regularization,
\item GRACE score refinement based on pooled gradient cosine similarity.
\end{enumerate}

At a high level, the gate first proposes dense teacher weights from pooled
student hidden states. A short hard-routing warmup keeps all teachers
available; after that, top-\(k\) and capacity constraints make the routing
sparse. Each routed teacher then contributes a candidate KD loss and a GRACE
score that measures directional agreement with the supervised CE update.

Before GRACE warmup, the method uses uniform KD weights over the teachers that
remain after routing. After warmup, the GRACE scores are smoothed with an EMA,
converted to a score softmax, blended with the routed weights, and replaced by
a uniform distribution only when the score spread is too small to justify a
strong preference.

The current repository objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{CE}
+
(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{KD}
+
\lambda_{bal}\mathcal{L}_{bal}
+
\lambda_{ent}\mathcal{L}_{ent}
+
\lambda_{z}\mathcal{L}_{z},
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda_{bal}\), \(\lambda_{ent}\), and \(\lambda_{z}\) are optional
router regularization weights.

This design is closer in spirit to selective gradient-based distillation \cite{zhu2021studentcustomized} and sparse expert routing \cite{shazeer2017outrageouslylargeneuralnetworks,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion} than to static teacher averaging.

Here it is adapted to the multi-teacher multimodal setting with token-level logit distillation.

Figure~\ref{fig:grace-overview} gives the full component view before the later
sections define each stage more precisely.

\subsection{Overall Training Flow}

The training step follows four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run the student to obtain \((\mathbf{z}_s,\mathbf{H}_s,\mathcal{L}_{CE})\).
\item Compute gate logits \(\mathbf r\), soft routing weights \(\mathbf p\), then route teachers with \(k_t=N\) during hard-routing warmup and \(k_t=k\) afterward.
\item Compute routed per-teacher ULD losses \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\). Before GRACE warmup, use uniform weights over the routed set.
\item After GRACE warmup, compute pooled cosine scores, smooth them with EMA, form \(q_i^{(b)}=\mathrm{softmax}(\beta \hat s_i^{(b)})\), blend \(q_i^{(b)}\) with \(\hat p_i^{(b)}\), and use uniform fallback when the score spread is below \(\epsilon\).
\end{enumerate}

The final KD loss is \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}=\sum_b\sum_{i\in\mathcal R_b} w_i^{(b)}\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\), and the full objective remains the sum of CE, routed KD, and optional router regularizers. In the current main
configuration reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation-methodology}, the key
GRACE-specific hyperparameters are \(k=2\), capacity factor \(\phi=1.25\),
balance weight \(\lambda_{bal}=0.01\), bias-update rate \(\eta_b=5\times10^{-4}\),
hard-routing warmup ratio \(\rho_h=0.2\), GRACE warmup ratio \(\rho_g=0.2\),
GRACE score softmax temperature \(\beta=20\), router-blend exponent
\(\lambda=0.5\), score-spread fallback threshold \(\epsilon=0.01\), logged
alignment threshold \(\delta=-0.02\), and KD coefficient \(\alpha=0.5\).

\section{Token-Level Distillation with ULD}

\subsection{Supervised Answer Tokens}

Distillation is applied only on supervised answer tokens, not on prompt tokens.
For sample \(b\), let \(\Omega_b\) denote the set of supervised token positions after:
\begin{enumerate}
\item masking ignored labels,
\item optionally removing the EOS token from both student and teacher answer spans,
\item truncating student and teacher answer spans to a common length.
\end{enumerate}

This produces aligned token sequences for the student and each teacher. The alignment is important because
teacher and student models may differ in tokenizer and vocabulary size.

\subsection{Universal Logit Distillation}

For each sample \(b\), token position \(j \in \Omega_b\), and teacher \(T_i\), let
\(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_s}\) be the student logits and
\(\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_i}\) be the teacher logits.
The student and teacher probability distributions are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{s,j}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}}{\tau_s}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{p}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}}{\tau_t}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_s\) and \(\tau_t\) are the student and teacher temperatures.

Following Universal Logit Distillation (ULD) \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation}, we sort both probability vectors in descending order and pad the smaller vocabulary with zeros.

Let \(\pi(\cdot)\) denote descending sort after zero-padding to a common size. The token-level ULD loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell_{ULD}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}, \mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right)
=
\left\|
\pi\!\left(\mathbf{p}_{s,j}^{(b)}\right)
-
\pi\!\left(\mathbf{p}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right)
\right\|_1.
\end{equation}

The per-sample, per-teacher KD loss is the mean over supervised answer tokens:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}
=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_b|}
\sum_{j \in \Omega_b}
\ell_{ULD}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}, \mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right).
\label{eq:per_teacher_uld}
\end{equation}

ULD is particularly suitable here because it does not require the student and teacher to share the same vocabulary \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation}.

This matters in our setting because the teacher pool may contain VLMs from different model families.

Figure~\ref{fig:grace-uld-sort-pad} visualizes the sort-pad-compare pipeline
behind Equation~\ref{eq:per_teacher_uld}.

\section{Learned Teacher Gate and Load Balancing}

\subsection{Student-Conditioned Router}

When the teacher pool contains multiple models, the method first builds a student-conditioned router instead of averaging teachers uniformly.

Let \(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\) denote the student hidden states captured at the routing source layer for sample \(b\). We pool these hidden states over supervised answer positions to obtain a routing feature
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}^{(b)} = \mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}

A deep router \(G(\cdot)\) then produces gate logits
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}^{(b)} = G\!\left(\mathbf{h}^{(b)}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{p}^{(b)} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\mathbf{r}^{(b)}\right),
\label{eq:router-softmax}
\end{equation}
where \(p_i^{(b)}\) is the initial gate weight for teacher \(T_i\).



\subsection{Top-k, Capacity, and Fallback}

The router is made sparse by applying top-\(k\) routing to bias-adjusted scores
\begin{equation}
\tilde{r}_i^{(b)} = r_i^{(b)} + b_i,
\end{equation}
where \(b_i\) is a teacher-specific expert bias. Let
\(\mathcal{K}_b=\mathrm{TopK}(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)},k)\) be the candidate
teacher set for sample \(b\). To prevent one teacher from receiving too many
assignments, each teacher is additionally limited by a capacity
\begin{equation}
C = \left\lceil \phi \frac{Bk}{N}\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
where \(B\) is the mini-batch size, \(N\) is the number of teachers, and
\(\phi > 0\) is the capacity factor.

Let \(m_i^{(b)} \in \{0,1\}\) denote the resulting assignment mask after top-\(k\)
selection and capacity pruning. The constrained gate weights are
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{m_i^{(b)} p_i^{(b)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} m_j^{(b)} p_j^{(b)}}.
\label{eq:constrained_gate_weights}
\end{equation}

If capacity pruning leaves a sample without any assigned teacher, the algorithm falls back to the teacher with the largest bias-adjusted routing score \(\tilde{r}_i^{(b)}\).

This ensures that at least one teacher remains available before GRACE refinement.

The current implementation also uses a short hard-routing warmup. Let
\(k_t\) denote the effective top-\(k\) value at training step \(t\), and let
\(\rho_h\) be the hard-routing warmup ratio. During the first \(\rho_h T\)
steps, the router uses
\begin{equation}
k_t = N,
\end{equation}
so all teachers remain available. After that point, the router switches to the
configured sparse value \(k_t = k\).

\subsection{Balancing Loss and Expert Bias Update}

The full GRACE framework can optionally regularize the gate with a balancing
loss that uses both soft routing weights and hard assignments.

Define the soft load
\begin{equation}
\bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} p_i^{(b)}
\end{equation}
and the hard assignment load
\begin{equation}
\bar{q}_i
=
\frac{1}{Bk}
\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\sum_{j=1}^{k}
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{TopK}_j(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)}) = i\right].
\end{equation}
The balancing objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{bal}
=
N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{p}_i\bar{q}_i.
\label{eq:balance_loss}
\end{equation}

When enabled, the expert bias is updated from the per-batch assignment load
\(\ell_i\) to discourage teacher overuse:
\begin{equation}
b_i \leftarrow b_i - \eta_b \left(\ell_i - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\ell_j\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_b\) is a small bias-update rate.

Two small auxiliary router penalties can also be enabled in the current code.
The first is a negative-entropy term
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{ent}
=
-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} H(\mathbf{p}^{(b)}),
\end{equation}
which discourages premature routing collapse. The second is a router \(z\)-loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{z}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\left(\log \sum_{i=1}^{N} e^{r_i^{(b)}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
which keeps router logits numerically well behaved.

\section{GRACE Score Refinement}

\subsection{Gradient Proxies}

For each sample \(b\), let \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}^{(b)}\) be the student cross-entropy loss on supervised answer tokens.
We define the CE logit-gradient proxy as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}_{CE}^{(b)}
=
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}} \mathcal{L}_{CE}^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

In implementation, this gradient is obtained in closed form from the student
probabilities and labels, which avoids a second backward pass through the full
model.

For each teacher \(T_i\), the KD logit-gradient proxy is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{(b)}
=
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\end{equation}

This proxy is computed directly from the selected distillation loss. In the
default setting of this thesis, \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\) is the ULD loss in
Equation~\ref{eq:per_teacher_uld}.

\subsection{GRACE Scores and Logged Active Flags}

For each sample \(b\) and teacher \(T_i\), we measure directional agreement between supervised learning and KD by cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_{CE}^{(b)}\right),
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_{i}^{(b)}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
s_i^{(b)}
=
\cos\!\left(
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)},
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}
\right).
\end{equation}

Here, \(\mathrm{Pool}(\cdot)\) averages the logit-gradient tensor over supervised
answer positions, producing one pooled CE gradient and one pooled KD gradient per
teacher. This is the quantity used by the current implementation.

The current implementation also records a thresholded activity flag
\begin{equation}
a_i^{(b)} = \mathbf{1}[s_i^{(b)} > \delta],
\end{equation}
where \(\delta\) is a logging threshold. These flags are useful for monitoring,
but the GRACE weight update itself uses the continuous scores \(s_i^{(b)}\).

\subsection{Warmup, Score Smoothing, and Gradient Weights}

GRACE refinement is enabled only after a warmup phase. Let \(\rho_g \in [0,1]\)
denote the GRACE warmup ratio with respect to the total number of training
steps. During the first \(\rho_g T\) steps, the current code uses a uniform KD
distribution over the teachers that remain after routing:
\begin{equation}
u_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\mathbf{1}[\,\hat{p}_i^{(b)} > 0\,]}
{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}[\,\hat{p}_j^{(b)} > 0\,]}.
\label{eq:uniform_routed_weights}
\end{equation}

After warmup, GRACE refines the routed teachers with a score-smoothed gradient
distribution. Let \(\bar{s}_i^{\mathrm{prev}}\) be the running exponential
moving average (EMA) of past batch means for teacher \(T_i\), and let \(\gamma\)
be the EMA decay. The per-sample smoothed score is
\begin{equation}
\hat{s}_i^{(b)}
=
\gamma \bar{s}_i^{\mathrm{prev}}
+
(1-\gamma) s_i^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/grace_ema_smoothing.png}
    \caption[EMA smoothing in GRACE.]{EMA smoothing in \methodname{}. The current batch score is blended with the running teacher score history before the GRACE softmax is applied. This damps batch-level noise and keeps the final gradient weights more stable.}
    \label{fig:grace-ema-smoothing}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:grace-ema-smoothing} explains why GRACE uses EMA before the
score softmax: raw per-batch scores are noisy, while smoothed scores produce
more stable teacher weights.

Let \(\mathcal{R}_b=\{i \mid \hat{p}_i^{(b)} > 0\}\) be the routed teacher set.
GRACE converts the smoothed scores into a gradient-weight vector
\begin{equation}
q_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{
\exp(\beta \hat{s}_i^{(b)}) \mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{R}_b]
}{
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\exp(\beta \hat{s}_j^{(b)}) \mathbf{1}[j \in \mathcal{R}_b]
},
\label{eq:grace_softmax}
\end{equation}
where \(\beta\) controls how sharply the highest-scoring teacher is preferred.

\subsection{Router-Gradient Blend and Uniform Fallback}

After warmup, GRACE combines the routed weights and the gradient weights by a
geometric blend. Let \(\lambda \in [0,1]\) be the router-blend exponent. The
unnormalized blended weights are
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_i^{(b)}
\propto
\left(\hat{p}_i^{(b)}\right)^{\lambda}
\left(q_i^{(b)}\right)^{1-\lambda},
\end{equation}
followed by normalization over the routed teachers:
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\tilde{w}_i^{(b)} \mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{R}_b]}
{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_j^{(b)} \mathbf{1}[j \in \mathcal{R}_b]}.
\label{eq:effective_teacher_weights}
\end{equation}

The implementation uses a uniform fallback only when the smoothed scores are too
close to justify a strong preference. Define the score spread
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{(b)}
=
\max_{i \in \mathcal{R}_b}\hat{s}_i^{(b)}
-
\min_{i \in \mathcal{R}_b}\hat{s}_i^{(b)}.
\end{equation}
If \(\Delta^{(b)} < \epsilon\), then GRACE falls back to
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\mathbf{1}[i \in \mathcal{R}_b]}
{|\mathcal{R}_b|}.
\label{eq:alignment_fallback}
\end{equation}
This keeps KD stable when the routed teachers have nearly indistinguishable
GRACE scores.

\subsection{Sample-Wise KD Aggregation}

The routed KD loss for sample \(b\) is the weighted aggregation
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i^{(b)} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\label{eq:sample_kd_average}
\end{equation}

The batch-level KD loss is then the mean over samples:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}.
\label{eq:batch_kd}
\end{equation}

This rule has two stages: the gate defines the routed teacher set, and GRACE
refines those routed teachers into the final KD weights.

\section{Final Objective}

The final optimization objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{CE}
+
(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{KD}
+
\lambda_{bal}\mathcal{L}_{bal}
+
\lambda_{ent}\mathcal{L}_{ent}
+
\lambda_{z}\mathcal{L}_{z},
\label{eq:final_objective}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\) is the standard supervised next-token cross-entropy loss and
\(\mathcal{L}_{KD}\) is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:batch_kd}. The balance term
\(\mathcal{L}_{bal}\) is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:balance_loss}; the entropy
term \(\mathcal{L}_{ent}\) and router \(z\)-loss \(\mathcal{L}_{z}\) are small
optional penalties on the teacher gate.

Equation~\ref{eq:final_objective} keeps the student anchored to ground-truth
supervision while adding routed KD and small router regularizers. Compared with
static multi-teacher averaging, this objective makes the teacher contribution
adaptive at the sample level. Compared with fixed teacher-subset selection, it
avoids committing to one subset for the whole run
\cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,zhu2021studentcustomized}.
