\section{Method Overview and Problem Formulation}

We consider text-rich multimodal understanding tasks such as TextVQA, DocVQA, and ChartQA
\cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.
The training set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}=\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{B},
\end{equation}
where \(x_b\) is a multimodal input and \(y_b\) is the target answer sequence.

Let \(S_{\theta}\) denote the student VLM with trainable parameters \(\theta\), and let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}=\{T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_N\}
\end{equation}
be a pool of frozen teacher VLMs. For each sample \(b\), the student produces logits
\(\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}\), and teacher \(T_i\) produces teacher logits
\(\mathbf{z}_t^{(b,i)}\).

The goal of this thesis is not to search for a fixed teacher subset before
training. Instead, the student is trained with
\emph{\methodfull{} (\methodname)}.

\methodname{} is organized around four interacting mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a supervised student objective \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\),
\item a per-teacher logit distillation objective \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\),
\item a deep teacher gate with top-\(k\) sparsification and capacity control,
\item optional balancing and expert-bias regularization for the router,
\item a cosine gradient-alignment filter with thresholding and least-negative fallback applied on top of the routed teachers.
\end{enumerate}

At a high level, each teacher first produces a candidate KD signal for the current sample.

The student-side gate proposes sparse routing weights from pooled hidden
features. When balancing is enabled, it regularizes the gate against collapse.
Gradient alignment removes routed teachers whose KD direction is not
sufficiently consistent with the supervised objective.

If all routed teachers fall below the alignment threshold, the method keeps the
least-negative routed teacher as a fallback. The final student update is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{CE}+\alpha \mathcal{L}_{KD}+\lambda_{bal}\mathcal{L}_{bal},
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha \in [0,1]\) controls the KD weight and \(\lambda_{bal}\)
controls the optional gate-balancing term.

This design is closer in spirit to selective gradient-based distillation \cite{zhu2021studentcustomized} and sparse expert routing \cite{shazeer2017outrageouslylargeneuralnetworks,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion} than to static teacher averaging.

Here it is adapted to the multi-teacher multimodal setting with token-level logit distillation.

\subsection{Overall Training Flow}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption[GRACE training procedure]{\methodfull{} (\methodname)}
\label{alg:training_procedure}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require mini-batch \(\{(x_b, y_b)\}_{b=1}^B\), frozen teachers \(\{T_i\}_{i=1}^N\)
\State Run \(S_\theta\) to obtain student logits, hidden states, and \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\)
\State Pool student hidden states and compute gate scores
\State Apply top-\(k\) routing, capacity control, and routing fallback
\For{each routed teacher \(T_i\)}
    \State Compute teacher logits and the per-teacher KD loss
\EndFor
\State Compute CE and KD gradient proxies, then alignment scores
\If{warmup is finished}
    \State Keep routed teachers with alignment score above \(\delta\)
    \If{none survive}
        \State Keep the least-negative routed teacher as fallback
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State Aggregate routed KD, add optional balance loss, and update \(\theta\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:training_procedure} gives the full training flow before the
sections below define each component in detail. In the current main
configuration reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation-methodology}, the key
GRACE-specific hyperparameters are \(k=1\), capacity factor \(\phi=4.0\),
balance weight \(\lambda_{bal}=0.0\), bias-update rate \(\eta_b=0.0\),
alignment warmup ratio \(\rho=0.2\), alignment threshold \(\delta=-0.02\), and
KD weight \(\alpha=0.5\).

\section{Token-Level Distillation with ULD}

\subsection{Supervised Answer Tokens}

Distillation is applied only on supervised answer tokens, not on prompt tokens.
For sample \(b\), let \(\Omega_b\) denote the set of supervised token positions after:
\begin{enumerate}
\item masking ignored labels,
\item optionally removing the EOS token from both student and teacher answer spans,
\item truncating student and teacher answer spans to a common length.
\end{enumerate}

This produces aligned token sequences for the student and each teacher. The alignment is important because
teacher and student models may differ in tokenizer and vocabulary size.

\subsection{Universal Logit Distillation}

For each sample \(b\), token position \(j \in \Omega_b\), and teacher \(T_i\), let
\(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_s}\) be the student logits and
\(\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{V_i}\) be the teacher logits.
The student and teacher probability distributions are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{s,j}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}}{\tau_s}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{p}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}
=
\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}}{\tau_t}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_s\) and \(\tau_t\) are the student and teacher temperatures.

Following Universal Logit Distillation (ULD) \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation}, we sort both probability vectors in descending order and pad the smaller vocabulary with zeros.

Let \(\pi(\cdot)\) denote descending sort after zero-padding to a common size. The token-level ULD loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell_{ULD}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}, \mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right)
=
\left\|
\pi\!\left(\mathbf{p}_{s,j}^{(b)}\right)
-
\pi\!\left(\mathbf{p}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right)
\right\|_1.
\end{equation}

The per-sample, per-teacher KD loss is the mean over supervised answer tokens:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}
=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_b|}
\sum_{j \in \Omega_b}
\ell_{ULD}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_{s,j}^{(b)}, \mathbf{z}_{t,j}^{(b,i)}\right).
\label{eq:per_teacher_uld}
\end{equation}

ULD is particularly suitable here because it does not require the student and teacher to share the same vocabulary \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation}.

This matters in our setting because the teacher pool may contain VLMs from different model families.

\section{Learned Teacher Gate and Load Balancing}

\subsection{Student-Conditioned Router}

When the teacher pool contains multiple models, the method first builds a student-conditioned router instead of averaging teachers uniformly.

Let \(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\) denote the student hidden states captured at the routing source layer for sample \(b\). We pool these hidden states over supervised answer positions to obtain a routing feature
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}^{(b)} = \mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{H}_s^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}

A deep router \(G(\cdot)\) then produces gate logits
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}^{(b)} = G\!\left(\mathbf{h}^{(b)}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{p}^{(b)} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\mathbf{r}^{(b)}\right),
\label{eq:router-softmax}
\end{equation}
where \(p_i^{(b)}\) is the initial gate weight for teacher \(T_i\).



\subsection{Top-k, Capacity, and Fallback}

The router is made sparse by applying top-\(k\) routing to bias-adjusted scores
\begin{equation}
\tilde{r}_i^{(b)} = r_i^{(b)} + b_i,
\end{equation}
where \(b_i\) is a teacher-specific expert bias. Let
\(\mathcal{K}_b=\mathrm{TopK}(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)},k)\) be the candidate
teacher set for sample \(b\). To prevent one teacher from receiving too many
assignments, each teacher is additionally limited by a capacity
\begin{equation}
C = \left\lceil \phi \frac{Bk}{N}\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
where \(B\) is the mini-batch size, \(N\) is the number of teachers, and
\(\phi > 0\) is the capacity factor.

Let \(m_i^{(b)} \in \{0,1\}\) denote the resulting assignment mask after top-\(k\)
selection and capacity pruning. The constrained gate weights are
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{m_i^{(b)} p_i^{(b)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} m_j^{(b)} p_j^{(b)}}.
\label{eq:constrained_gate_weights}
\end{equation}

If capacity pruning leaves a sample without any assigned teacher, the algorithm falls back to the teacher with the largest bias-adjusted routing score \(\tilde{r}_i^{(b)}\).

This ensures that at least one teacher remains active before alignment filtering.

\subsection{Balancing Loss and Expert Bias Update}

The full GRACE framework can optionally regularize the gate with a balancing
loss that uses both soft routing weights and hard assignments.

Define the soft load
\begin{equation}
\bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} p_i^{(b)}
\end{equation}
and the hard assignment load
\begin{equation}
\bar{q}_i
=
\frac{1}{Bk}
\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\sum_{j=1}^{k}
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{TopK}_j(\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(b)}) = i\right].
\end{equation}
The balancing objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{bal}
=
N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{p}_i\bar{q}_i.
\label{eq:balance_loss}
\end{equation}

When enabled, the expert bias is updated from the per-batch assignment load
\(\ell_i\) to discourage teacher overuse:
\begin{equation}
b_i \leftarrow b_i - \eta_b \left(\ell_i - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\ell_j\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_b\) is a small bias-update rate.

\section{Gradient Alignment Filtering}

\subsection{Gradient Proxies}

For each sample \(b\), let \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}^{(b)}\) be the student cross-entropy loss on supervised answer tokens.
We define the CE logit-gradient proxy as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}_{CE}^{(b)}
=
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}} \mathcal{L}_{CE}^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

In implementation, this gradient is obtained in closed form from the student probability distribution and target labels,
which avoids an additional backward pass through the full model.

For each teacher \(T_i\), the KD logit-gradient proxy is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}_{i}^{(b)}
=
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_s^{(b)}} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\end{equation}

This proxy is computed directly from the selected distillation loss.
In the default setting of this thesis, \(\mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}\) is the ULD loss in
Equation~\ref{eq:per_teacher_uld}.

\subsection{Alignment Score}

For each sample \(b\) and teacher \(T_i\), we measure directional agreement between supervised learning and KD by cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_{CE}^{(b)}\right),
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}
=
\mathrm{Pool}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_{i}^{(b)}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
s_i^{(b)}
=
\cos\!\left(
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{CE}^{(b)},
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{(b)}
\right).
\end{equation}

Here, \(\mathrm{Pool}(\cdot)\) averages the logit-gradient tensor over supervised
answer positions, producing one pooled CE gradient and one pooled KD gradient per
teacher. This is the quantity used by the current implementation.

\subsection{Warmup, Thresholding, and Fallback}

The alignment filter is enabled only after an optional warmup phase. Let
\(\rho \in [0,1]\) denote the warmup ratio with respect to the total number of
training steps. During the first \(\rho\) fraction of training, the method uses
only the constrained gate weights \(\hat{p}_i^{(b)}\). After warmup, a teacher is
considered alignment-active when
\begin{equation}
a_i^{(b)} = \mathbf{1}[s_i^{(b)} > \delta],
\label{eq:alignment_active}
\end{equation}
where \(\delta\) is the gradient-alignment threshold.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/grace_warmup_schedule.png}
    \caption[Warmup schedule in GRACE.]{Warmup schedule used by \methodname{}. During the early stage, the method trusts the routed weights \(\hat{p}_i^{(b)}\) directly. After the warmup boundary \(\rho T\), the alignment filter is activated and the final effective weights depend on both routing and alignment.}
    \label{fig:grace-warmup}
\end{figure}

After warmup and thresholding have identified the alignment-active teachers,
the alignment filter is applied on top of the already routed teachers. If at
least one routed teacher satisfies Equation~\ref{eq:alignment_active}, the
final effective teacher weights are
\begin{equation}
w_i^{(b)}
=
\frac{\hat{p}_i^{(b)} a_i^{(b)}}
{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{p}_j^{(b)} a_j^{(b)}}.
\label{eq:effective_teacher_weights}
\end{equation}

If thresholding removes all routed teachers, \methodname{} does not drop KD for
that sample. Instead, it falls back to the routed teacher with the largest
alignment score:
\begin{equation}
i^\star
=
\arg\max_{i:\hat{p}_i^{(b)}>0} s_i^{(b)},
\qquad
a_i^{(b)} = \mathbf{1}[i=i^\star].
\label{eq:alignment_fallback}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:effective_teacher_weights} is then recomputed with this fallback
mask. As a result, the current implementation never drops KD entirely for a sample
after routing; instead, it keeps the least-negative routed teacher when necessary.

\subsection{Sample-Wise KD Aggregation}

The routed KD loss for sample \(b\) is the weighted aggregation
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i^{(b)} \mathcal{L}_{KD}^{(b,i)}.
\label{eq:sample_kd_average}
\end{equation}

The batch-level KD loss is then the mean over samples:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{KD}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{KD}^{(b)}.
\label{eq:batch_kd}
\end{equation}

This rule has two stages: the gate proposes sparse teacher weights, and
gradient alignment filters the routed teachers before the final weighted KD
average is formed.

\section{Final Objective}

The final optimization objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{CE}
+
\alpha \mathcal{L}_{KD}
+
\lambda_{bal}\mathcal{L}_{bal},
\label{eq:final_objective}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{L}_{CE}\) is the standard supervised next-token cross-entropy loss and
\(\mathcal{L}_{KD}\) is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:batch_kd}. The balance term
\(\mathcal{L}_{bal}\) is defined in Equation~\ref{eq:balance_loss} and is used only
when \(\lambda_{bal}>0\).

Equation~\ref{eq:final_objective} keeps the student anchored to ground-truth
supervision while adding routed KD and, when enabled, an auxiliary balancing
term. Compared with static multi-teacher averaging, this objective makes the
teacher contribution adaptive at the sample level. Compared with fixed
teacher-subset selection, it avoids committing to one subset for the whole run
\cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,zhu2021studentcustomized}.
