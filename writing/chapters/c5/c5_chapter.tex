\subsection{Overall Comparison}

Tables~\ref{tab:textvqa-comparison}, \ref{tab:docvqa-comparison}, and \ref{tab:chartqa-comparison} summarize the main comparison results across original models, direct fine-tuning, standard logits distillation baselines, and \methodname.

Missing entries are marked as `N/A' when a run was not completed or not reported in the current experiment set.

We report the baselines in four groups.

\begin{itemize}
\item The \textbf{non-distillation references} are the original student and the full-finetuning baseline.
\item The \textbf{same-family baselines} use \textbf{SmolVLM2-2.2B-Instruct} as teacher for forward KL, reverse KL, Jensen-Shannon divergence, and HSM-CKA \cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge,dasgupta2025improving}.
\item The \textbf{different-family single-teacher baselines}\\
use a teacher outside the SmolVLM family. This group includes the ULD baseline \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation} and the layer-wise hidden-state baseline.
\item The \textbf{heterogeneous multi-teacher baselines} use teacher pools drawn from different VLM families, including uniform averaging, reinforced selection, and \methodname{} \cite{williams1992simple,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,yu2024selectdistill}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Representation Similarity Analysis}

We use CKA as an analysis tool to examine whether teacher and student layers
exhibit clear correspondence. For centered hidden-state matrices
\(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s\) and \(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t\), linear CKA is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CKA}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t)=
\frac{\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s\right\|_F^2}
{\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t\right\|_F
\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s\right\|_F}.
\end{equation}
Values closer to \(1\) indicate stronger representation similarity
\cite{kornblith2019similarityrepresentations}.

The main question is whether strong internal similarity is enough to justify
layer-wise distillation. Our results suggest that it is not, as shown in the
figures and tables below.

Figures~\ref{fig:exp-cka-multi-dataset} and
\ref{fig:exp-cka-vision-multi-dataset} show that the teacher and student can be
relatively close in both the language and vision stacks.

However, Tables~\ref{tab:textvqa-comparison}, \ref{tab:docvqa-comparison}, and
\ref{tab:chartqa-comparison} still show a clear benchmark gap between compact
students and stronger models. The layer-wise baseline also fails to
consistently outperform output-level distillation.

This supports the design choice in GRACE. High hidden-state similarity alone is
not a reliable signal that direct layer matching will produce better task
transfer.
