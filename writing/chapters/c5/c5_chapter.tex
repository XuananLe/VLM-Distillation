\subsection{Overall Comparison}

Tables~\ref{tab:textvqa-comparison}, \ref{tab:docvqa-comparison} and \ref{tab:chartqa-comparison} summarize the main comparison results across original models, direct fine-tuning, standard logits distillation baselines and \methodname.

Unless stated otherwise, the main comparison tables report the strongest completed run for each method and benchmark. Uniform, SHM-CKA, REINFORCE and \methodname{} use a four-teacher mixed set spanning the Qwen, InternVL and Gemma families. Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation-methodology} documents the active four-teacher DocVQA launch script for system-level reproducibility.

We report the baselines in four groups.

\begin{itemize}
\item The \textbf{non-distillation references} are the original student and the full-finetuning baseline used for comparison.
\item The \textbf{same-family baselines} use \textbf{SmolVLM2-2.2B-Instruct} as teacher for forward KL, reverse KL and Jensen-Shannon divergence \cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge,dasgupta2025improving}.
\item The \textbf{different-family single-teacher baselines}\\
use a teacher outside the SmolVLM family. This group includes the ULD baseline \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation}.
\item The \textbf{multi-teacher baselines} compare four-teacher Uniform averaging with adaptive methods such as SHM-CKA, REINFORCE and \methodname{} \cite{williams1992simple,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,yu2024selectdistill}.
\end{itemize}

Because each multi-teacher run requires large offline logit caches and long
single-GPU training, we report one fully trained run per method. A typical H200
run takes about 7 hours (about 14.41 USD at 2.059 USD/hour on Vast.ai
before storage and offline-logit generation cost). We therefore treat small differences
cautiously and focus on consistent multi-point gaps across datasets and student
sizes.

Several trends are consistent across the three benchmarks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Same-family logits baselines remain weak. Forward KL gives only small gains, reverse KL is consistently poor and Jensen-Shannon is unstable. Simply matching a same-family teacher distribution is not enough for these compact students.
    \item \textbf{Cross-family ULD is already stronger than the same-family logits losses on all three tasks.} This shows that cross-tokenizer distillation is more useful here than vocabulary-matched KL. Once multiple teachers are added, Uniform averaging gives another clear jump, which means teacher diversity itself is already valuable.
    \item \textbf{The additional gain from the single-teacher ULD baseline to \methodname{} comes from the full recipe: a trie-Wasserstein KD loss, sample-dependent sparse routing and gradient-agreement refinement after routing.}
    \item \textbf{\methodname{} improves on the uniform multi-teacher baseline for both student sizes.} The gain is moderate on TextVQA and DocVQA, but larger on ChartQA, indicating that adaptive weighting helps most when the task mixes visual reading, numerical reasoning and teacher specialization.
\end{itemize}

For the qualitative analysis below, we use DEX-AR
\cite{bousselham2026dexar}. For token \(t\), layer \(l\) and head \(h\),
DEX-AR forms a gradient-based attribution map
\[
\mathbf{G}_{t}^{(l,h)}
=
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}^{(l,h)}} y_t \odot \mathbf{A}^{(l,h)},
\]
then keeps visually grounded heads with
\[
w^{(l,h)}=\max\!\big(S_{\mathrm{img}}^{(l,h)}-S_{\mathrm{text}}^{(l,h)},0\big),
\]
and aggregates
\[
\mathbf{H}_t=\sum_{l,h} w^{(l,h)} \mathbf{G}_{t}^{(l,h)},
\qquad
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{sent}}=\sum_t \delta_t \mathbf{H}_t .
\]
Here \(\delta_t=1/|\Omega_{\mathrm{ans}}|\) for answer tokens
\(t\in\Omega_{\mathrm{ans}}\) and \(\delta_t=0\) otherwise, so the
sentence-level map is the uniform average of token-level maps over the
generated answer span.
We prefer DEX-AR over raw attention maps or generic saliency because the model
is autoregressive and the target is answer-token grounding, not generic image
saliency.

\textbf{The gain is not merely an artifact of comparison with weak baselines.} On two
benchmarks, \methodname{} removes about half of the original teacher-student
gap. Even on DocVQA, where the teacher ceiling is highest, it still closes
almost 40\% of the gap.

\textbf{The student can even outperform some individual teachers in the pool.}
On TextVQA, \methodname{} reaches 69.38 and exceeds Gemma-3-4B-it at 57.80 by
+11.58 points (+20.03\%). On ChartQA, it exceeds InternVL3-1B at 75.30 by
+4.90 points (+6.51\%) and Gemma-3-4B-it at 63.60 by +16.60 points (+26.10\%).
This shows that the student is not simply bounded by the weaker teachers
it learns from.

\textbf{One negative result is also consistent: reverse KL is much weaker than
forward KL.} The more mode-seeking objective is a poor fit
for these compact multimodal students.

\textbf{SHM-CKA is not enough by itself.} SHM-CKA uses the heterogeneous
teacher set, but replaces routed weighting with hidden-state matching. It is
competitive on ChartQA, but clearly weaker on TextVQA and DocVQA. Representation
alignment alone is not sufficient.
Teacher assignment matters, especially when the pool contains teachers with
different strengths and tokenizers.

\textbf{A clear limitation remains on the vision side.} The method
distills supervised answer-token logits, pools student hidden states for
routing and scores teachers from CE/KD agreement at the output side. This is
effective, but it does not explicitly align visual features, cross-attention
patterns, or region-level evidence inside the VLM. The method therefore treats
the vision encoder mostly through its effect on the final answer distribution,
not as a first-class distillation target.

\section{Representation Similarity Analysis}

We use Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) as an analysis tool to examine whether teacher and student layers
exhibit clear correspondence. For centered hidden-state matrices
\(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s\) and \(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t\), linear CKA is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CKA}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t)=
\frac{\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s\right\|_F^2}
{\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t\right\|_F
\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_s\right\|_F}.
\end{equation}
Values closer to \(1\) indicate stronger representation similarity
\cite{kornblith2019similarityrepresentations}.

The main question is whether strong internal similarity is enough to justify
layer-wise distillation. Our results suggest that it is not, as shown in the
figures and tables below.

\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}
This section answers RQ2 from Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction} by isolating the
main components of \methodname{} on DocVQA. Table~\ref{tab:docvqa-planned-ablation}
keeps the teacher set and KD loss close to the final method and changes one
mechanism at a time:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cross-family ULD (single teacher)} uses one cross-family teacher and optimizes \(\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{CE}+\alpha_{\mathrm{KD}}\mathcal{L}_{ULD}\), with \(\mathcal{L}_{ULD}=\|\pi(p_s)-\pi(p_t)\|_1\). This provides the single-teacher cross-tokenizer baseline.
\item \textbf{Uniform averaging} averages the active per-teacher KD losses,
\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{CE}
+
\alpha_{\mathrm{KD}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{L}_{TW}^{(i)}.
\]
This tests whether teacher diversity alone explains the gain.
\item \textbf{Routing only + trie-Wasserstein} keeps sparse teacher selection but replaces post-routing weights with uniform weights, so \(w_i^{(b)}=u_i^{(b)}=\mathbf{1}[i\in\mathcal{R}_b]/|\mathcal{R}_b|\). This tests whether routing alone explains the gain.
\item \textbf{Full GRACE + trie-Wasserstein} is the full method and serves as the reference row, with \(w_i^{(b)}\propto (\hat p_i^{(b)})^{\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}}}(\omega_i^{(b)})^{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}}}\).
\item \textbf{Full GRACE + trie-Wasserstein (\(\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}} = 1.0\))} reduces the final weights to router weights only, \(w_i^{(b)}=\hat p_i^{(b)}\) after normalization over routed teachers. This tests whether post-routing blending matters.
\item \textbf{Full GRACE + trie-Wasserstein (\(\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}}=0\))} removes EMA smoothing, so \(\hat{s}_i=s_i\) and \(\omega_i^{(b)}=\mathrm{Softmax}(\beta_{\mathrm{score}}s_i)\) over the active routed set. This tests whether temporal score smoothing stabilizes teacher weighting.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:docvqa-planned-ablation} shows that the full method is the
strongest setting for both student sizes. Three findings stand out.

\textbf{Teacher diversity is necessary but not sufficient.}
Moving from a single cross-family teacher (ULD) to uniform four-teacher
averaging adds +3.46 on SmolVLM-256M and +4.31 on SmolVLM-500M. The gain
confirms that multiple heterogeneous teachers provide better supervision than
any single teacher, but uniform weighting leaves a further gap of +4.78/+3.52
to the full method.

\textbf{Routing without gradient refinement can hurt.}
On SmolVLM-500M, routing-only scoring (63.23) is \textit{worse} than uniform
averaging (69.59) by $-6.36$ points. The router selects teachers but assigns
them incorrect relative weights; without GRACE, these wrong weights degrade
the result below a naive equal average. On SmolVLM-256M the pattern reverses:
routing-only (57.45) beats uniform (55.70) by +1.75. The larger student
extracts stronger routing features from its hidden states, so its router
produces a sharper but less accurate ranking that amplifies errors when
uncorrected. The smaller student's weaker routing signal is closer to uniform,
so the damage is smaller.

\textbf{Gradient-agreement refinement is the critical corrective layer.}
Full GRACE recovers +9.88 points over routing-only on SmolVLM-500M and +3.03
on SmolVLM-256M. Decomposing the 500M gain: the router-only blend
($\lambda_{\mathrm{blend}}\!=\!1.0$) already recovers +7.22 points
(63.23~$\to$~70.45) by using learned router weights instead of uniform
post-routing weights, and gradient-agreement scoring adds another +2.66
(70.45~$\to$~73.11). On SmolVLM-256M, the router-only blend adds only +0.52
(57.45~$\to$~57.97), while gradient scoring adds +2.51
(57.97~$\to$~60.48). Gradient-agreement refinement therefore contributes
consistently across both student sizes, whereas the router's own weight
quality depends on student capacity.

Removing EMA smoothing ($\beta_{\mathrm{EMA}}\!=\!0$) degrades both students
(256M: 60.48~$\to$~57.45, 500M: 73.11~$\to$~71.25), confirming that temporal
smoothing stabilizes score estimates but is secondary to the refinement step
itself.

\begin{rqanswerbox}{Answer to RQ2}
The ablation isolates three cumulative contributions: (1)~heterogeneous
teacher diversity (+3 to +4 pts over single-teacher ULD), (2)~sparse routing
structure (beneficial only when paired with refinement) and
(3)~gradient-agreement refinement, which adds +3 to +10~pts over
routing-only and is the single largest contributor. EMA smoothing provides
additional stabilization (+1 to +3~pts). Without gradient refinement, routing
can degrade performance below uniform averaging.
\end{rqanswerbox}

\section{Efficiency Analysis}
This section addresses RQ3 from Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction}. Runtime,
training cost and storage overhead are analyzed using the H200 rental rate and
the observed Cloudflare R2 storage footprint.

\begin{rqanswerbox}{Answer to RQ3}
The cost of \methodname{} is dominated by GPU training rather than object
storage. From the Vast.ai screenshot, one H200 instance costs 2.059 USD/hour,
so a 7-hour run costs about \(7\times2.059=14.41\) USD and a 2500 USD budget
corresponds to
\[
\frac{2500}{2.059} \approx 1214.18 \text{ GPU-hours},
\]
which is about 50.59 days of continuous single-GPU runtime. If one full run
takes about 7 hours, this budget is equivalent to roughly
\[
\frac{1214.18}{7} \approx 173
\]
single-H200 runs.

Storage costs are much lower under the official Cloudflare R2 standard
pricing policy: 0.015 USD/GB-month for storage, 4.50 USD per million Class A
operations, 0.36 USD per million Class B operations and zero egress. Using the
dashboard values in Figure~\ref{fig:r2-storage}, 1.04 TB of stored logits is
about 1040 GB, which gives
\[
1040 \times 0.015 = 15.60 \text{ USD/month}.
\]
The 598.73k Class A operations stay below the 1M monthly free allowance, so
their marginal cost is 0 USD. The 20.3M Class B operations exceed the 10M free
allowance by 10.3M, which gives
\[
10.3 \times 0.36 = 3.71 \text{ USD}.
\]
The total R2 cost is therefore about
\[
15.60 + 3.71 = 19.31 \text{ USD/month},
\]
before billing-unit rounding. This is below 1\% of the 2500 USD GPU budget,
which means the main efficiency bottleneck is training compute and offline
logit generation, not storage egress or serving cost.
\end{rqanswerbox}

\section{Discussion}
The results show a clear ordering across method families. Same-family KL
baselines are weakest, single cross-family distillation is stronger, uniform
multi-teacher weighting improves further and \methodname{} is best overall
\cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation}.
No single component fully addresses the heterogeneous distillation problem:
heterogeneous teachers provide better supervision, but the student still
needs a cross-tokenizer loss, sample-dependent routing and a stable
post-routing weighting scheme.

The training recipe explains this pattern. It uses a shared-trie Wasserstein
loss for mismatched tokenizers, a routed multi-teacher setup with top-\(k\)
masking, routed-weight renormalization and gradient-agreement refinement with
EMA-smoothed score updates
\cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,peyre2019computationaloptimaltransport,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion}.
The smoother training curve in Figure~\ref{fig:training-curve-comparison} is
consistent with that design: the router narrows the teacher set first, then
GRACE reweights only the routed teachers.

The largest gain appears on ChartQA, and the trend holds for the smaller
256M student as well. Sample-dependent teacher assignment is most useful when
the task mixes visual parsing and numerical reasoning, and the method also
benefits the more resource-constrained student.

The comparison with SHM-CKA reinforces this conclusion. SHM-CKA uses the
heterogeneous teacher set but replaces routed weighting with hidden-state
matching; it is competitive on ChartQA yet clearly weaker on TextVQA and
DocVQA. Together with the CKA heatmaps, this confirms that internal
similarity alone does not guarantee effective transfer.
\textbf{Sample-dependent teacher weighting remains necessary}
\cite{kornblith2019similarityrepresentations,dasgupta2025improving}.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cost-limited evaluation.} The reported numbers are single-run results. A typical H200 run takes about 7 hours and costs about 14.41 USD at 2.059 USD/hour on Vast.ai before offline cache-generation and storage cost. This limits repeated seeds, confidence intervals and broader hyperparameter sweeps, so small gaps between strong baselines should be interpreted cautiously.
    \item \textbf{Limited sweep coverage.} The main tables use a shared four-teacher pool for Uniform, SHM-CKA, REINFORCE and \methodname{}, but the study still reports single completed runs and does not exhaustively sweep seeds, teacher subsets or hyperparameters. This does not invalidate the overall trend, but it limits strict uncertainty estimates.
    \item \textbf{Answer-token-centric transfer.} The method distills teacher distributions on supervised answer positions, routes teachers from pooled student states and refines them with CE/KD gradient agreement. It does not explicitly align visual tokens, cross-attention maps or region-level evidence. As a result, some gains may come mainly from output-side transfer instead of deeper visual grounding.
    \item \textbf{Systems dependence.} The implementation relies on large offline teacher-logit caches, remote object storage and local streaming caches. This keeps the online loop simple, but it raises storage, I/O and reproducibility requirements. The method is therefore easier to reproduce in a well-provisioned training environment than on minimal local hardware.
    \item \textbf{Scope of validation.} The empirical study focuses on text-rich VQA benchmarks and compact students in the SmolVLM family. The reported gains are strong in this regime, but the thesis does not yet establish the same behavior for other multimodal task families, other student architectures or larger teacher pools.
\end{itemize}
