\section*{Summary}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Summary}
This thesis studied knowledge distillation for heterogeneous vision-language
models, where teacher utility varies across samples and teacher-student
tokenizers do not match. Building on cross-tokenizer distillation, optimal
transport and sparse routing
\cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,peyre2019computationaloptimaltransport,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion},
the work introduced \gracefull{} (\gracename{}), a routed multi-teacher
framework that combines three main contributions: trie-Wasserstein
tokenizer-agnostic distillation, sparse teacher routing and gradient-agreement
refinement.

The first contribution is a geometry-aware cross-tokenizer distillation
objective. Trie-Wasserstein KD maps decoded teacher and student tokens into a
shared byte-trie structure and compares subtree probability masses, so token
distributions can be matched across different vocabularies. The second
contribution is an objective-aware post-routing refinement mechanism. Sparse
routing selects sample-relevant teachers, while gradient-agreement refinement
adjusts routed teacher weights according to alignment between each teacher's KD
gradient and the supervised answer gradient. The third contribution is an
empirical study showing that output-level tokenizer-agnostic distillation with
routing and gradient agreement outperforms the compared same-family,
single-teacher and heterogeneous multi-teacher baselines in the evaluated VQA
setting.

Across TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA, \methodname{} obtains the highest scores
among the compared methods in Chapter~\ref{chap:experiment-results}. For
SmolVLM-500M, it reaches 69.38 on TextVQA, 73.11 on DocVQA and 80.20 on
ChartQA. For SmolVLM-256M, it reaches 60.73, 60.48 and 72.32 on the same three
benchmarks. On SmolVLM-500M, \methodname{} closes about 55\% of the original
teacher-student gap on TextVQA and ChartQA and about 40\% on DocVQA.

\section*{Limitations}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Limitations}
Despite these results, several limitations remain that future work should
address. \textbf{Cost-limited evaluation} is the first limitation. The reported
numbers are single-run results, so the thesis does not provide confidence
intervals or repeated-seed estimates for small gaps between high-scoring
baselines. As shown in
Section~\ref{sec:efficiency-analysis}, an average run costs approximately
14.41 USD before offline cache generation and storage cost. Although the main
tables use a shared four-teacher pool and consistent hyperparameters, the
absence of repeated seeds limits strict uncertainty claims about closely
matched methods.

\textbf{Scope of validation} is also limited. The empirical study focuses on
VQA benchmarks and SmolVLM-family students. The results support text-rich VQA
distillation with compact projector-based students, but they do not establish
whether the same behavior holds for other multimodal task families, other
student architectures or larger teacher pools. Tasks with long-form answers,
multi-image inputs, video reasoning or open-ended dialogue may place different
demands on teacher selection and answer-token supervision.

\textbf{Reasoning-oriented models are not explicitly supported.} GRACE
currently distills teacher supervision through answer-token output
distributions and gradient agreement with the supervised answer loss. This
design is appropriate for the evaluated VQA setting, where the target is a
short final answer. It does not yet explicitly support reasoning-oriented or
``thinking'' models whose behavior depends on intermediate deliberation traces,
hidden reasoning tokens or extended test-time reasoning. If a teacher produces
only a final answer distribution, GRACE can still use that distribution as a
teacher signal. However, the method does not distinguish between reasoning-phase
tokens and answer-phase tokens, does not route teachers based on the quality of
intermediate reasoning and does not define a distillation objective for
multi-step thought trajectories.

\section*{Future Work}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Future Work}
Future work should extend the thesis in four directions. First, stronger
evaluation should use repeated runs with multiple random seeds, report mean and
standard deviation and use paired comparisons when the same cached teacher
logits are reused. This would make the smaller differences between close
multi-teacher baselines easier to interpret.

Second, systems work should reduce the storage, I/O and gradient-computation
costs of heterogeneous teacher distillation. More compact logit storage,
adaptive cache loading and lower-cost agreement scoring would make the method
easier to scale to more datasets and larger teacher pools.

Third, future method extensions should move beyond answer-token-centric
distillation. Visual-token alignment, cross-attention transfer and region-level
matching could complement output-level KD, especially for tasks where the
student must localize evidence before producing a short answer.

Fourth, support for reasoning-oriented models should be studied with
process-level distillation mechanisms. Such extensions would need to separate
reasoning-stage information from final-answer generation and define how
teachers are routed or weighted when intermediate reasoning traces are
available. Future work can also explore richer router inputs, improved
objective-conflict handling and teacher pools that mix specialized document,
chart, general VLM and reasoning-oriented experts
\cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,yu2024selectdistill}.
