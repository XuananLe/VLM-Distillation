\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

\section*{Motivation}

\textbf{Vision-language models (VLMs)} link a vision encoder, a multimodal
connector and an autoregressive language decoder to read text embedded in
images, interpret document layouts, parse charts and answer questions about
visual scenes
\cite{alayrac2022flamingovisuallanguagemodel,li2023blip2bootstrappinglanguageimagepretraining,liu2023visualinstructiontuning,singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

Neural scaling laws push the most capable VLMs toward larger language backbones
and more extensive multimodal training, consistently improving performance at the
cost of compute and memory
\cite{kaplan2020scalinglaws,hoffmann2022trainingcomputeoptimal}. Current frontier
models make this tension concrete: Qwen2.5-VL-72B, NVLM-D 1.0 72B, Meta's
Llama 3.2 Vision 90B and Llama 3-V 405B represent the upper range of open-weight
and open-access VLMs
\cite{bai2025qwen25vl,dai2024nvlm,meta2024llama32,grattafiori2024llama3}.
In bfloat16 inference, storing weights alone requires roughly \(2P\) bytes for
\(P\) parameters, placing these models at approximately 144 GB, 180 GB and
810 GB before key-value cache, activations and runtime overhead, well above the
80 GB of a single H100 \cite{nvidia2024h100}. Serving them therefore requires
multi-GPU configurations, raising latency, energy cost and infrastructure
complexity. These deployment barriers extend beyond research: robotics, embodied
AI, mobile applications, document-processing pipelines and enterprise systems all
impose real-time, cost and security constraints that the largest models cannot
meet \cite{driess2023palme,brohan2023rt2}.

\textbf{Knowledge distillation} addresses this tension by transferring knowledge
from a large teacher model to a compact student. Rather than learning from raw
annotations alone, the student is trained against the teacher's output
distributions and intermediate representations, which carry soft probability
signals over alternative answers and provide richer supervision than one-hot labels
\cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge}.
Distillation has produced compact models that retain most of their teachers'
accuracy, with recent lightweight VLMs reaching competitive benchmark scores
under resource constraints
\cite{chu2023mobilevlm,zhou2024tinyllava,marafioti2025smolvlm}.

\section*{Research Gaps and Proposed Approach}

No single approach resolves all of these constraints simultaneously. Response-based
distillation aligns teacher and student output distributions effectively but
requires a shared tokenizer
\cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge}.
Transport-based methods remove this constraint by comparing probability mass
over decoded strings, yet they are typically formulated for single-teacher transfer,
leaving sample-level teacher selection outside their scope
\cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,takezawa2021supervisedtreewasserstein,leyamadafukumizucuturi2019treesliced}.
Representation matching through hidden states or CKA provides a
tokenizer-independent signal but does not calibrate answer probabilities or
resolve conflicts between teachers
\cite{kornblith2019similarityrepresentations}. Multi-teacher distillation improves by pooling supervision from several teachers;
however, fixed or globally learned weights can mix helpful teacher outputs with
weak or conflicting ones at the sample level
\cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,yu2024selectdistill}.

These gaps are most acute for text-heavy visual tasks, including document QA,
chart interpretation and scene-text understanding, where teachers from different
families tokenize the same answer strings differently and complement each other
across task types. Although recent open-source VLMs have
narrowed the gap to proprietary systems on standard benchmarks
\cite{wang2024qwen2vl,chen2024internvl15,chen2024internvl25}, compact models
still struggle when fine visual details, document structure and numerical
relations must all be captured. To address these constraints, this thesis proposes and evaluates
\textbf{\gracefull{} (\gracename{})}, a heterogeneous multi-teacher VLM
distillation framework that combines \textbf{tokenizer-agnostic} distribution
matching, \textbf{sample-dependent} teacher selection and \textbf{gradient-aware
conflict resolution}. Three research questions guide the evaluation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1: Performance.} How does \methodname{} compare
with direct fine-tuning, same-family logit-based KD, ULD and
heterogeneous multi-teacher baselines on the target VQA benchmarks?
\item \textbf{RQ2: Component Analysis.} How much do trie-Wasserstein KD,
sparse top-\(k\) routing and gradient-agreement refinement contribute to
the final performance?
\item \textbf{RQ3: Efficiency.} What runtime overhead does \methodname{}
introduce during training?
\end{itemize}

\section*{Contributions}

This thesis makes three main contributions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Byte-Trie Wasserstein distillation.}
    We propose a Byte-Trie Wasserstein KD loss that maps teacher and
    student tokens onto a shared UTF-8 byte trie, comparing byte paths
    rather than vocabulary indices to resolve tokenizer mismatch.

    \item \textbf{Objective-aware multi-teacher weighting.}
    We introduce a mechanism for weighting routed teachers where top-\(k\) routing
    selects candidate teachers and gradient agreement filters their KD signals
    against the supervised CE objective.

    \item \textbf{Empirical validation on VQA benchmarks.}
    We evaluate the full framework on TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA with two
    SmolVLM students and a heterogeneous four-teacher pool. The results show
    that \methodname{} outperforms fine-tuning, same-family KD, cross-family ULD
    and multi-teacher baselines, while ablations show that routing is
    unreliable until gradient-agreement refinement is added.
\end{itemize}

Evaluated with VLM-Eval-Kit on TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA, \methodname{}
with SmolVLM-500M as student closes 55\%, 40\% and 55\% of the original
teacher-student gap on the three benchmarks.

\section*{Thesis Organization}

The remainder of this thesis is structured as follows. Chapter~\ref{chap:preliminaries} provides background on VLMs, knowledge
distillation, distribution matching across mismatched tokenizers, sparse routing and
gradient conflict resolution. Chapter~\ref{chap:proposed-method} presents
\methodname{} in full, covering the Byte-Trie Wasserstein objective, sparse teacher
router, gradient-agreement refinement and combined training objective.
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation-methodology} describes the experimental setup,
datasets and evaluation metrics, baselines and ablation settings.
Chapter~\ref{chap:experiment-results} reports the main comparison, ablation
study, efficiency analysis and discussion. A concluding chapter summarizes the main findings, identifies three limitations of the current evaluation and outlines four future directions: repeated-run evaluation, reduced teacher-logit storage and I/O cost, visual token alignment and reasoning-oriented distillation.
