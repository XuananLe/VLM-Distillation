\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and Motivation}
The core task in this thesis is to distill strong multimodal ability from large
VLM teachers into \textbf{sub-billion} VLM students. The goal is to do so
without losing performance on text-centric benchmarks. Vision-Language Models
(VLMs) jointly process images and text for tasks such as image captioning,
visual question answering, document understanding and instruction following
\cite{radford2021learningtransferablevisualmodels,alayrac2022flamingovisuallanguagemodel,li2023blip2bootstrappinglanguageimagepretraining,liu2023visualinstructiontuning,hu2024visualprogramdistillation}.

In this thesis, \textit{multimodal understanding} refers to practical capabilities such as reading text in images, understanding the input question and performing basic reasoning over visual and textual evidence, not broad general intelligence \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}. Our focus is efficient transfer of this ability to compact students.

However, high-performing VLMs are often large and expensive in memory, latency and deployment cost \cite{alayrac2022flamingovisuallanguagemodel,li2023blip2bootstrappinglanguageimagepretraining,chu2023mobilevlm,zhou2024tinyllava}. This cost limits their use in resource-constrained settings.

This pressure is consistent with scaling-law results. Kaplan et al. show that
model quality improves predictably with scale, while Hoffmann et al. show that
compute-optimal training still requires jointly increasing model size and data
tokens \cite{kaplan2020scalinglaws,hoffmann2022trainingcomputeoptimal}. Larger
models therefore remain attractive for accuracy, but they do not become cheap
to train or deploy.

    \label{fig:intro-scaling-laws}
\end{figure}

The vision side makes this constraint sharper in VLMs. VLMs insert visual embeddings or
visual tokens into the multimodal context together with text, so
the effective sequence length grows with image tokenization density. Since
Transformer attention scales quadratically with sequence length, increasing the
visual token budget quickly raises memory and latency \cite{vaswani2023attentionneed,liu2023visualinstructiontuning,marafioti2025smolvlm}. For compact VLMs, this means that shrinking only the language backbone is not enough. The visual stack and the number of visual tokens also need to be controlled.

For this thesis, the most important regime is \textbf{small models}, especially
\textbf{sub-billion VLMs}. Models in the 1B to 3B range are already cheaper
than large 7B to 70B systems, but they can still be heavy for edge deployment,
memory-limited serving, or repeated evaluation across many checkpoints.
Sub-billion students are a stricter target. They force the model to use its
parameter budget and visual tokens more carefully, while still aiming to retain
useful multimodal performance \cite{chu2023mobilevlm,zhou2024tinyllava,marafioti2025smolvlm}.

Knowledge distillation (KD) transfers knowledge from a large teacher to a
compact student, often preserving strong performance at lower cost
\cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge}.

Figure \ref{fig:intro-small-vlm-tradeoff} shows why this direction matters. Compact multimodal models can already be competitive, but closing the gap to stronger models still requires better transfer of knowledge.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro-closed-open-gap} sharpens this point. On these three
benchmarks, the best open-source models are already close to the best
proprietary models and in two cases they are ahead.

This makes white-box distillation especially attractive. In black-box KD, the
student can only query an external teacher service and read its returned
predictions. In white-box KD, the student can also use teacher logits and
intermediate states \cite{wang2021blackboxkd,ma2024blackboxkd}. That extra
access enables objectives such as the trie-Wasserstein KD loss used in this thesis, as well as prior baselines such as ULD and hidden-state matching, which are not
available in a pure black-box setting
\cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,takezawa2021supervisedtreewasserstein,dasgupta2025improving,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge}.

Another major competing compression path is quantization. Unlike distillation,
quantization compresses an already trained model by reducing numerical
precision. It can cut memory and improve inference speed substantially, so it
is a serious practical alternative to KD
\cite{frantar2022gptq,xiao2023smoothquant,lin2024awq}.

In VLMs, however, naive low-bit quantization can hurt multimodal accuracy
\cite{lin2024awq,li2025mbq}. For this reason, we treat quantization as a
complementary deployment step, not a substitute for better transfer during
training.

This observation is exactly why we focus on \textbf{sub-billion vision-language
models}. They are small enough to make the efficiency constraint real, yet
strong enough that improvements from better distillation remain meaningful
\cite{marafioti2025smolvlm}.

In the multimodal setting, recent work has shown that distillation can transfer
reasoning or representation knowledge into more efficient VLMs
\cite{fang2021compressingvlm,hu2024visualprogramdistillation,yang2024clipcid}.
This is especially relevant on text-rich benchmarks such as TextVQA, DocVQA,
and ChartQA
\cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro-knowledge-distillation} summarizes the teacher-student
transfer pattern that this thesis extends to the heterogeneous multi-teacher
setting.

Since no single teacher excels across all multimodal tasks, \textit{multi-teacher distillation} can provide richer supervision than a single-teacher pipeline \cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,yu2024selectdistill}.

In our setting, this is not just a matter of picking several strong teachers. The teacher pool contains heterogeneous VLMs with different tokenizers, different output vocabularies and different sample-level strengths \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning}.

A naive uniform average can therefore mix teachers that help one sample but hurt another \cite{xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning}.

This motivates a selective multi-teacher framework over static teacher averaging.

We introduce \methodfull{} (\methodname{}), a selective multi-teacher distillation framework that routes teachers per sample, applies a trie-Wasserstein cross-tokenizer loss and refines teacher weights with gradient-based alignment. The framework tackles tokenizer mismatch, heterogeneous teacher specialization and post-routing instability in one recipe. The next section formalizes the problem and requirements.

\section{Problem Statement and Research Objectives}
This thesis addresses one central problem: how to transfer multimodal
understanding from large VLMs to compact students. The transfer must preserve
task performance while cutting deployment cost.

We focus on text-rich multimodal tasks where the student must read image text, understand the question and perform basic reasoning \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

In this setting, existing distillation pipelines often assume a single teacher, a shared tokenizer, or a fixed averaging rule across teachers. These assumptions are restrictive for heterogeneous multi-teacher VLM distillation \cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning}.

More specifically, the method should satisfy three requirements.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The KD loss should remain valid even when teacher and student tokenizers differ.
\item Teacher assignment should be \emph{sample-dependent}, not fixed across the whole dataset.
\item The routing mechanism should remain sparse while suppressing routed teachers whose KD direction conflicts with the supervised objective. When needed, the framework should also support regularizers that discourage routing collapse.
\end{enumerate}

Given a pool of \(N\) frozen teacher VLMs and a compact student, the goal is therefore to learn a distillation strategy that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Remains valid across mismatched teacher and student vocabularies.
\item Routes teachers adaptively from student-side features through a learned student-conditioned gate.
\item Retains sparse top-\(k\) computation under capacity constraints.
\item Filters routed teachers when their KD update direction conflicts with the supervised objective, while keeping a fallback teacher when necessary.
\end{enumerate}

This thesis addresses these requirements with \methodfull{} (\methodname).

\methodname{} is a selective multi-teacher distillation framework.

It combines a trie-Wasserstein cross-tokenizer KD loss, a student-conditioned teacher gate, top-\(k\) capacity-aware sparse routing and gradient-based GRACE score refinement. The framework also supports routing fallback after capacity pruning, uniform fallback when score spread is too small and balancing and expert-bias updates \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,peyre2019computationaloptimaltransport,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion,zhou2022mixtureofexpertswithexpertchoice,zhu2021studentcustomized}.

We evaluate it on text-rich multimodal understanding tasks such as TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

Accordingly, this thesis focuses on cross-tokenizer multimodal distillation for compact students and evaluates \methodname{} on TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA, with analysis of routing behavior and representation matching.

\section{Thesis Contributions}
The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We define a heterogeneous multi-teacher distillation setting for compact VLMs with mismatched teacher and student vocabularies on TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA.
\item We present \methodfull{} (\methodname{}), a selective multi-teacher distillation framework that combines trie-Wasserstein cross-tokenizer KD, a student-conditioned gate, sparse top-\(k\) routing and GRACE score refinement.
\item We show that this framework directly addresses the main practical failures of heterogeneous VLM distillation: tokenizer mismatch, sample-dependent teacher conflict and unstable teacher weighting after routing.
\item We establish consistent gains over fine-tuning and strong distillation baselines, supported by routing, ablation and representation analyses.
\end{enumerate}
