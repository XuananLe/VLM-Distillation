\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

\section{Background and Motivation}
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) jointly process images and text for tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering, document understanding and instruction following \cite{radford2021learningtransferablevisualmodels,alayrac2022flamingovisuallanguagemodel,li2023blip2bootstrappinglanguageimagepretraining,liu2023visualinstructiontuning,hu2024visualprogramdistillation}.

In this thesis, \textit{multimodal understanding} refers to practical capabilities such as reading text in images, understanding the input question and performing basic reasoning over visual and textual evidence, rather than broad general intelligence \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

    \centering

    \caption{CLIP-style vision-language pretraining aligns image and text representations in a shared embedding space \cite{wikimedia_clip_pretraining_image,radford2021learningtransferablevisualmodels}.}
    \label{fig:intro-vlm-pretraining}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:intro-vlm-pretraining} illustrates the basic vision-text
pretraining pattern that underlies many modern VLM families.

However, high-performing VLMs are often large and expensive in memory, latency, and deployment cost \cite{alayrac2022flamingovisuallanguagemodel,li2023blip2bootstrappinglanguageimagepretraining,chu2023mobilevlm,zhou2024tinyllava}. This limits their use in resource-constrained settings.

This pressure is consistent with scaling-law results. Kaplan et al. show that
model quality improves predictably with scale, while Hoffmann et al. show that
compute-optimal training still requires jointly increasing model size and data
tokens \cite{kaplan2020scalinglaws,hoffmann2022trainingcomputeoptimal}. Larger
models therefore remain attractive for accuracy, but they do not become cheap
to train or deploy.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.84\linewidth]{figures/kaplan_scaling_law_chart.png}
    \caption[Scaling-law trends from Kaplan et al.]{Scaling-law trends from Figure 1 of Kaplan et al. The reported loss decreases smoothly as compute, dataset size, and parameter count increase, which explains why larger teachers remain attractive even when deployment favors smaller students \cite{kaplan2020scalinglaws}.}
    \label{fig:intro-scaling-laws}
\end{figure}

The vision side makes this constraint sharper in VLMs. Visual embeddings or
visual tokens are inserted into the multimodal context together with text, so
the effective sequence length grows with image tokenization density. Since
Transformer attention scales quadratically with sequence length, increasing the
visual token budget quickly raises memory and latency \cite{vaswani2023attentionneed,liu2023visualinstructiontuning,marafioti2025smolvlm}. For compact VLMs, this means that shrinking only the language backbone is not enough. The visual stack and the number of visual tokens also need to be controlled.

For this thesis, the most important regime is \textbf{small models}, especially
\textbf{sub-billion VLMs}. Models in the 1B to 3B range are already cheaper
than large 7B to 70B systems, but they can still be heavy for edge deployment,
memory-limited serving, or repeated evaluation across many checkpoints.
Sub-billion students are a stricter target. They force the model to use its
parameter budget and visual tokens more carefully, while still aiming to retain
useful multimodal performance \cite{chu2023mobilevlm,zhou2024tinyllava,marafioti2025smolvlm}.

The issue is especially important for text-centric tasks such as scene-text reading, document understanding, and chart reasoning \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

Knowledge distillation transfers knowledge from a large teacher to a compact student, often preserving strong performance at lower cost \cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge}.

Figure \ref{fig:intro-small-vlm-tradeoff} shows why this direction matters. Compact multimodal models can already be competitive, but closing the gap to stronger models still requires better transfer of knowledge.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro-closed-open-gap} sharpens this point. On these three
benchmarks, the best open-source models are already close to the best
proprietary models, and in two cases they are ahead.

This makes white-box distillation especially attractive. In black-box KD, the
teacher is treated as an inaccessible service and the student can rely only on
returned predictions, whereas white-box settings permit direct use of logits
and intermediate states \cite{wang2021blackboxkd,ma2024blackboxkd}. That extra
access enables objectives such as ULD and hidden-state matching, which are not
available in a pure black-box setting
\cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,dasgupta2025improving,xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge}.

Another major competing compression path is quantization. Unlike distillation,
quantization compresses an already trained model by reducing numerical
precision. It can cut memory and improve inference speed substantially, so it
is a serious practical alternative to KD
\cite{frantar2022gptq,xiao2023smoothquant,lin2024awq}.

In VLMs, however, naive low-bit quantization can hurt multimodal accuracy
\cite{lin2024awq,li2025mbq}. For this reason, we treat quantization as a
complementary deployment step rather than a substitute for better transfer
during training.

This observation is exactly why we focus on \textbf{SmolVLM-500M} and
\textbf{SmolVLM-256M}. They are small enough to make the efficiency constraint
real, yet strong enough that improvements from better distillation remain
meaningful \cite{marafioti2025smolvlm}.

In the multimodal setting, recent work has shown that distillation can transfer
reasoning or representation knowledge into more efficient VLMs
\cite{fang2021compressingvlm,hu2024visualprogramdistillation,yang2024clipcid}.
This is especially relevant on text-rich benchmarks such as TextVQA, DocVQA,
and ChartQA
\cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro-knowledge-distillation} summarizes the teacher-student
transfer pattern that this thesis extends to the heterogeneous multi-teacher
setting.

Since no single teacher excels across all multimodal tasks, \textit{multi-teacher distillation} can provide richer supervision than a single-teacher pipeline \cite{yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning,yu2024selectdistill}.

In our setting, this is not just a matter of picking several strong teachers. The teacher pool contains heterogeneous VLMs with different tokenizers, different output vocabularies, and different sample-level strengths \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning}.

A naive uniform average can therefore mix teachers that help one sample but hurt another \cite{xu2024surveyknowledgedistillationlarge,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning}.

This motivates a selective multi-teacher framework rather than static teacher averaging.

The concrete problem and design requirements are stated in the next section.

\section{Problem Statement and Research Objectives}
The central problem addressed in this thesis is how to transfer multimodal understanding ability from large VLMs to compact student models without paying the full cost of large-scale deployment.

We focus on text-rich multimodal tasks where the student must read image text, understand the question, and perform basic reasoning \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

In this setting, existing distillation pipelines often assume a single teacher, a shared tokenizer, or a fixed averaging rule across teachers. These assumptions are restrictive for heterogeneous multi-teacher VLM distillation \cite{hinton2015distillingknowledgeneuralnetwork,boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,yuan2020reinforcedmultiteacherselection,zhang2023adaptivemultiteachermetalearning}.

More specifically, the method should satisfy three requirements.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The KD loss should remain valid even when teacher and student tokenizers differ.
\item Teacher assignment should be \emph{sample-dependent} rather than fixed across the whole dataset.
\item The routing mechanism should remain sparse while suppressing routed teachers whose KD direction conflicts with the supervised objective. When needed, the framework should also support regularizers that discourage routing collapse.
\end{enumerate}

Given a pool of \(N\) frozen teacher VLMs and a compact student, the goal is therefore to learn a distillation strategy that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item remains valid across mismatched teacher and student vocabularies,
\item routes teachers adaptively from student-side features through a learned deep gate,
\item retains sparse top-\(k\) computation under capacity constraints,
\item and filters routed teachers when their KD update direction conflicts with the supervised objective, while keeping a fallback teacher when necessary.
\end{enumerate}

This thesis addresses these requirements with \methodfull{} (\methodname).

\methodname{} is a selective multi-teacher distillation framework.

It uses tokenizer-agnostic Universal Logit Distillation (ULD), a deep student-conditioned teacher gate, top-\(k\) capacity-aware sparse routing, and gradient-based GRACE score refinement. The framework also supports routing fallback after capacity pruning, uniform fallback when score spread is too small, and optional balancing and expert-bias updates \cite{boizard2024towardscrosstokenizerdistillation,fedus2022switchtransformersscalingtrillion,zhou2022mixtureofexpertswithexpertchoice,zhu2021studentcustomized}.

We evaluate it on text-rich multimodal understanding tasks such as TextVQA, DocVQA, and ChartQA \cite{singh2019vqamodelsread,mathew2021docvqadatasetvqadocument,masry2022chartqabenchmarkquestionanswering}.

Accordingly, this thesis has three main objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
\item formulate cross-tokenizer multimodal distillation for compact VLM students in a text-centric benchmark setting,
\item design \methodname{} as a selective multi-teacher distillation framework with ULD, a deep student-conditioned gate, top-\(k\) capacity-aware routing, and gradient alignment, while supporting optional balancing and expert-bias updates,
\item evaluate whether \methodname{} improves transfer for small VLMs on TextVQA, DocVQA and ChartQA, and analyze why simple layer-wise transfer is less effective in this setting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis Contributions}
The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formulate a practical multi-teacher distillation setting for compact VLMs with heterogeneous teacher vocabularies on text-rich multimodal benchmarks.
\item We propose \methodfull{} (\methodname{}), a unified distillation framework for heterogeneous teachers. It uses Universal Logit Distillation, a deep student-conditioned routing gate, top-\(k\) capacity-aware sparse assignment, and GRACE score refinement, while also supporting optional balancing and expert-bias updates.
\item We provide empirical evidence on TextVQA, DocVQA, and ChartQA that this design yields stronger transfer than standard fine-tuning and conventional logits-based distillation baselines for compact VLM students.
\item We analyze representation similarity and layer-wise transfer, showing why high internal similarity alone is insufficient and why naive layer matching is a weak distillation bias in this setting.
\item We complement the headline comparison with routing and ablation analysis, clarifying how selective teacher assignment contributes to the final gains.
\end{enumerate}
